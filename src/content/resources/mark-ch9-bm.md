“IF YOU CAN”?
Mark 9:14-29
Key Verse: 9:23
“If you can”? said Jesus. “Everything is possible for him who believes.”
In today’s passage, Jesus laments over the unbelief of people by saying, “O
unbelieving generation.” He declares, “Everything is possible for him who believes.” What
does this mean? Does it mean Jesus will give us a red Ferrari if we believe in him? Let us
think about what kind of faith Jesus wants us to have.
I. WHAT ARE YOU ARGUING ABOUT? (14-18)
When Jesus went up on the transfiguration mountain, he took with him only three
disciples, Peter, James and John. Jesus sometimes invited only these three to observe
certain important events (Mk 5:37; 14:33). On the mountain, Jesus revealed his original
image as the Son of God. His face was shining like the sun. His clothes became dazzling
white. And Moses and Elijah, two great prophets from the Old Testament, appeared to talk
with Jesus. This was a spectacular display. Peter did not know that to say. He mumbled
something about building three bungalows on the mountain. He felt like he was in heaven.
He was so mesmerized by the glory of Jesus that he did not want to come down from the
mountain.
God woke him up from his fantasy by thundering from the cloud. He said, “This is
my son whom I love. Listen to him!” God told Peter to listen to Jesus. Peter did not want to
listen to Jesus, especially about his upcoming suffering and death. But God told him to
listen to Jesus. As they were coming down from the mountain, Jesus once again talked
with them about his upcoming suffering, death and resurrection. No one wants to talk
about suffering and death. But Jesus did so again and again. It is because the way of the
cross is the way of salvation for all sinners like you and me. We will live forever in heaven
only because Jesus suffered and died for us. This is the core of the gospel.
Meanwhile, an embarrassing scene was developing for the other nine disciples at
the foot of the mountain. Look at verses 14-15. When they came to the other disciples, they
saw a large crowd around them and the teachers of the law arguing with them. As soon
as all the people saw Jesus, they were overwhelmed with wonder and ran to greet him.
The nine disciples were arguing with the teachers of the law while a large crowd of people
were watching them with curiosity. The scene resembled a circus and the disciples looked
like clowns. Why? What were they arguing with the religious leaders about? It turned out
that a situation happened to the nine disciples while Jesus and the three were on the
mountain of transfiguration.
It happened this way. A man brought his boy possessed by an evil spirit, hoping
Jesus could heal his son. But Jesus was not there. Only the nine disciples were there. The
nine disciples at that time felt kind of left out, because Jesus did not take them. They were
probably jealous of the top three. When the man with a sick boy showed up, they decided

boy. They wanted to see another miracle performed by Jesus.
"What are you arguing with them about?" Jesus asked (16). The disciples remained
silent. They were too embarrassed to talk to him about the incident. Instead, a man in the
crowd stood up. It was the boy’s father. He said to Jesus, "Teacher, I brought you my son,
who is possessed by a spirit that has robbed him of speech" (17). He went on, “You know,
every morning my son said to me, ‘Good morning, Daddy! Have a good day at work,
Daddy! Don’t forget you promised to take me to bowling tonight.’ He’s the joy of my life
and the apple of my eye because he is my only son. Suddenly, he was possessed by an evil
spirit and he could not speak. When the evil spirit seizes him it throws him to the ground.
He foams at the mouth. I feel like dying looking at him. I cry every night. I have tried
everything to help him. Finally I brought him to your disciples. They made all kinds of
motions, but could not drive the evil spirit from my son.” It was a heart wrenching story. I
am sure Jesus was moved by the father’s heart toward his suffering son. He was not happy
about what the disciples did. But Jesus had bigger fish to fry. What was that?
II. O UNBELIEVING GENERATION (19)
Look at verse 19. “O unbelieving generation,” Jesus replied, “how long shall I stay
with you? How long shall I put up with you?” Jesus said, “O unbelieving generation.”
Jesus looked beyond the nine disciples’ inability to help the boy. Jesus saw a generation of
unbelieving people. Unbelieving people occupied the whole world. Men were unbelieving.
Women were unbelieving. Old people were unbelieving and young people were
unbelieving. Whenever they spoke, they spoke unbelieving words. People today may

consider unbelief not a serious problem. Many view problems such as recession, poverty
and climate change more serious than unbelief in God. To the father of the boy in today’s
passage, his son’s health was the most serious problem. Likewise, we may consider our
problems with health, jobs, finance and school to be more serious than unbelief. But Jesus
said, “O unbelieving generation.” In his eyes, the unbelief problem was more serious than
anything else.
Why is unbelief such a serious problem for mankind? Unbelieving people say that
they can do fine without God. They sometimes even blame God for the problems of the
world. Unbelieving people make decisions with no regard to God or his word. They believe
that the world would be better off without God. But unbelief is the main source of many
serious problems. Unbelieving people are cut from the source of their lives, the Creator
God. They may look good for now, just as the cut flowers in a vase look beautiful for a
while. The unbelieving people have no place in the eternal kingdom of God.
Unbelief also leads to many destructive behaviors on both the personal level and
also the society. St. Paul describes this in the second part of Romans 1. Because of
unbelieving hearts, their sinful desire leads them to sexual immorality with one another.
Because of their unbelieving hearts, they exchange the truth of God for a lie. They worship
and serve created things rather than the Creator. Because of their unbelieving hearts, they
become filled with every kind of wickedness and evil, greed and depravity. They are full of
envy, murder, strife, deceit, and malice, gossips, slanderers and god-haters; insolent,
arrogant and boastful. Because of their unbelieving hearts, they disobey their parents; they
are senseless, faithless, heartless, and ruthless. Although they know God, they pretend not
to know God. Because of their unbelieving hearts, they know their evildoing deserves
death, but they do not discontinue it (Ro 1:18-32).
Jesus lamented over the unbelieving generation. He saw the tragedy and miseries
that came from their unbelieving hearts. They were not pathetic because they were
occupied by the Roman Empire. They were pathetic because they were unbelieving people.
Jesus tried his best. He tried to plant the hope of the kingdom of God by teaching them the
word of God. He healed many sick people in order to let them appreciate God's love
through his healing ministry. But still, the world did not listen to him. It was an
unbelieving generation.
What would Jesus say to our generation? What would he say about people who
celebrate his birthday with Santa Claus and his resurrection with a bunny? What would he
say about people who spend more time on sports and entertainment than on his word and
prayer? I have a suspicion that he would lament over our generation as well, saying, “O
unbelieving generation!” Then, what can we do to change this?
III. EVERYTHING IS POSSIBLE FOR HIM WHO BELIEVES (20-27)
Jesus’ heart went out to the suffering boy. A boy who should be full of life ahead of
him was suffering greatly because of the evil spirit inside him. Jesus wanted to heal the
boy. But the sickness of unbelief was even more serious. This sickness was killing people’s
souls. So he turned to the boy’s father first. He wanted to heal the man’s sickness of

unbelief. Look at verse 21. Jesus asked the boy's father, “‘How long has he been like this?”
“From childhood,” he answered. Jesus had a great compassion on the father. Jesus
understood his agony. So he asked him, "How long has he been like this?" How did the
man answer? He said, "From childhood. It has often thrown him into fire or water to kill
him. But if you can do anything, take pity on us and help us" (22). His answer showed how
much he had been suffering because of his son. He was in deep despair. At the same time,
his answer showed his unbelief. He was talking to the Son of God, but all he could say was
“If you can do anything, take pity on us and help us.” He had no real faith in Jesus. He was
different from other sick people who had come forward to Jesus. A man with leprosy came
to Jesus and said, “Jesus, you can make me clean.” A woman with bleeding problem
believed that if only she touched the edge of Jesus’ cloak, she would be healed. A Roman
centurion said to Jesus, “Just say the word, and my servant will be healed.” These people
had faith in Jesus. But the boy’s father was unbelieving. He was part of the unbelieving
generation. How did Jesus help him?
Look at verse 23. “If you can”? said Jesus. “Everything is possible for him who
believes.” Jesus rebuked him. The boy’s father had no conviction. His faith was 50/50. He
brought his son to Jesus but did not believe Jesus could heal him with certainty. So Jesus
told him: “If you can? Everything is possible for him who believes.” What does this mean?
Of course, this does not mean we can make God a vending machine—getting things we
want on demand. It simply means that nothing is impossible for God, for he is the
Almighty God. It means that when we have faith in God, we can have everything we need
to serve him.
"Unbelieving" is a person's way of life that excludes God in his decisions. Unbelieving
is a way of thinking based on doubt and fear. Hebrews 11:6 says that without faith it is
impossible to please God. On the other hand, believing means to say, "I cannot do anything
by myself but I can do everything with God’s help." The apostle Paul declares in Philippians
4:13: "I can do everything through him who gives me strength." Paul said this when he was a
prisoner in Rome. He was an old man. He was in chains. But he believed that God could use
him to evangelize the world. So by faith he had a Bible study ministry in his prison.
Everything was possible for Paul who believed in God!
Look at verse 24. "Immediately the boy's father exclaimed, ‘I do believe; help me
overcome my unbelief!'" This father had suffered much and he was totally helpless. But
now because of Jesus, a beam of hope was shining in his heart. He prayed, “Help me
overcome my unbelief!” This is a good prayer we can adopt.
When Jesus saw that a crowd was running to the scene, he rebuked the evil spirit.
“You deaf and mute spirit,” he said, “I command you, come out of him and never enter
him again” (25). Look at the scene of the evil spirit running away. The spirit shrieked,
convulsed him violently and came out. The boy looked so much like a corpse that many
said, “He's dead.” An evil spirit never gives up his victim easily. He knew he had to come
out of the boy. But he made a scene. The evil spirit made the boy look dead.
Look at verse 27. "But Jesus took him by the hand and lifted him up to his feet, and
he stood up." Jesus drove out the evil spirit from the boy. He took him by the hand and

IV. ONLY BY PRAYER (28-29)
After Jesus had gone indoors, his disciples spoke to him privately. In a whisper,
they asked Jesus, “Why couldn't we drive it out?” Look at verse 29. He replied, “‘This kind
can come out only by prayer.” The work of God is what God does. We are only the
instrument that he uses. We don’t have the power. It comes from God. So we must pray.
We should not be proud, thinking that we can heal someone just because we have done it
before. Our prayer is telling God that we are weak but he is strong. We cannot do anything
alone unless he enables us. So prayer and faith go hand in hand.

Today's passage is very relevant to us. We live in an unbelieving generation. Jesus
would rebuke us, “If you can? Everything is possible for him who believes!” May God help
us to fight against unbelief in ourselves! May God help us to pray, “Help me overcome my
unbelief!” so that by faith we can do the good works that God prepared in advance for us to
do!
