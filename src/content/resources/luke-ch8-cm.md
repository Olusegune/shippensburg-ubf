WHERE IS YOUR FAITH?
Luke 8:22-25
Key Verse: 8:25
“Where is your faith?” he asked his disciples. In fear and amazement they asked
one another, “Who is this? He commands even the winds and the water, and they
obey him.”
In today’s passage, Jesus and his disciples meet a fierce storm at sea. They were
almost drowned. Our life is often compared to a voyage. We have to face many small and
big storms. How can we overcome our storms in life? Jesus points to one thing by asking
us the question, “Where is your faith?” Let us think about what this means.
First, “Let’s go over to the other side” (22-23). As this passage begins, Jesus and
his disciples set out to have a quiet retreat in a remote location. They had been working
hard to take care of the crowds of people who came to them day in and day out. They
had no time for themselves. Jesus knew that his disciples needed a short vacation. On
their way, however, they met an unexpected storm, and their vacation plan was
frustrated. But in the stormy sea, Jesus revealed himself--who he really is--and taught
them important spiritual lessons for the future leaders.
Training his disciples to raise them up to be spiritual leaders was the main focus
of Jesus’ ministry. From the outset of his ministry, Jesus chose the Twelve and began to
train them. Jesus had very busy daily life as the Messiah. Everywhere he went, a large
crowd gathered and demanded his attention. Frequently he and his disciples did not
even have time to eat (Mk 6:31). Because he spent so much time with the crowd, he
could use only bits and pieces of time for his disciples. Yet, he was always mindful of
them, teaching them spiritual lessons. By enduring many persecutions of the religious
leaders, he taught them the way of the cross. Most of all, he helped them open their
spiritual eyes to understand spiritual things, especially the secrets of the kingdom of
God. At the same time, in the midst of his busy schedule, he found time to pray—to have
a personal relationship with God. He would get up early in the morning and go to a
solitary place to have a quiet time. His daily routine was quite ordinary, but it was full of
grace and truth.
Look at verse 22. One day Jesus said to his disciples, “Let’s go over to the other
side of the lake.” So they got into a boat and set out. Jesus and his disciples were busy
everyday in taking care of the people who came to them. Jesus knew that his disciples
were on the verge of burning out. They needed a time to rest. They needed personal
fellowship with him, away from the crowd. One day, he said to his disciples, “Let us go
over to the other side.” What was on the other side? This sounds mysterious. But the
disciples understood what he meant; it was a short vacation. There was no crowd on the
other side of the lake. The twelve disciples were excited. They were happy to go on a
vacation with Jesus for the first time. Immediately they began to shout, “Let’s go!”

The sea was calm as they began the boat ride. Soon Jesus and his disciples were
in the middle of the sea. The gentle water of the sea splashed and sparkled along the
boat under the moonlight. The sound of their rowing was smooth and rhythmical
because some of them were seasoned fishermen. The boat was gliding on the calm
water. It was going to be a perfect vacation, they thought.
What did Jesus do while they were enjoying the boat ride? Look at verse 23a. As
they sailed, he fell asleep. Jesus was catching a nap in the stern of the boat. It was a rare
moment of quiet peace for Jesus. Then something happened to disturb their voyage.
Look at verse 23 again. As they sailed, he fell asleep. A squall came down on the lake, so
that the boat was being swamped, and they were in great danger.
Suddenly a squall came down on the lake. The Sea of Galilee is surrounded by
plateaus on the west and great mountains on the east. Rivers flowing from the
mountains create deep ravines. These ravines play the role of funnels, drawing down
cold wind from the mountains and thus causing sudden storms. Storms on the lake were
unpredictable.
Giant waves beat into the small boat, and water began to fill the bottom of it. The
boat was being swamped. Some of the disciples were seasoned sailors on the lake. They
knew every inch of the lake. They had braved many storms in the past. Using all their
skills, they tried to steer the boat through the storm. But it did not work. They lost
control. The waves began to dump a lot of water in the boat. They frantically bailed, but
it was a losing battle. They were in danger of drowning. Suddenly, all the terrible stories
they heard of the fishermen lost in the sea became real to them. Their vacation spirit
was gone. They began to panic. Meanwhile, Jesus had fallen into a deep sleep. Can you
imagine sleeping through a category 3 hurricane, with wind howling at 120 miles per
hour? His disciples were in panic. They didn’t know what to do in the life-and-death
situation. But Jesus slept soundly despite the commotion around him. He remained
calm. How can we remain calm in the midst of a storm? Let us find the answer to this
question as we continue to study this passage.
Second, “Where is your faith?” (24-25) Look at verse 24. The disciples went and
woke him, saying, “Master, Master, we’re going to drown!” Finally, the disciples
remembered that Jesus was with them in the boat. It dawned on them that Jesus could
help them. After all, they had seen how Jesus had done miraculous things such as
healing a quadriplegic on the spot and raising a dead young man from his coffin. So they
went to Jesus and registered their SOS or distress signal directly. It was their 911 call.
What did Jesus do when they woke him up? He got up and rebuked the wind and the
raging waters; the storm subsided, and all was calm. This was an amazing thing that
the disciples experienced personally. In fear and amazement they asked one another,
“Who is this? He commands even the winds and the water, and they obey him” (25b).
The most important lesson they learned from the experience was that Jesus is the
Lord of the universe. It taught them who Jesus is. He is the Creator God. Jesus can
control the nature. Jesus can control the winds and waves of the sea. No man ever
rebuked the raging storms of the sea. But Jesus did and they obeyed him. Jesus is the

Creator God who has authority over the nature. Julius Caesar was a great conqueror.
Once he met a storm on the voyage to a battle. He struggled hard to save his battleships
and finally fell down from exhaustion. He was a great conqueror in history, yet he was a
mere man before the angry nature. Human beings often become proud because of many
things they have achieved through science and technology. But many times they are
humbled by the nature. We heard the sad news that dozens of people were killed by a
massive earthquake in Kyusue, Japan. We saw pictures of people with terrifying looks
on their faces as they huddled on streets. Then just this morning, a stronger quake (7.8
magnitude) struck Ecuador, killing at least 77 people. We become helpless when we are
struck by natural disasters such as earthquakes, hurricanes, and floods. But to Jesus,
hurricanes, even the category 5 ones, are not a big problem. Our God is a great God. Our
God is the everlasting God. This event revealed that Jesus is the Creator God.
It is important for us to know that Jesus is the Lord God who created the heavens
and the earth. Genesis 1:1 says, “In the beginning God created the heavens and the
earth.” When we believe that God is the Creator of the heavens and the earth, he opens
our spiritual eyes to see his kingdom and his righteousness. We can be truly happy,
knowing that the Creator God is with us. John 1:3 says of Jesus: “Through him all things
were made; without him nothing was made that has been made.” If we have faith in this
Jesus, we don’t have to afraid even when storms of life rage around us. We can face the
storms knowing that Jesus is with us in our life boat and he will help us to overcome the
storms.
Jesus’ disciples were living together with Jesus and traveling with him for quite
some time. They sensed that Jesus was an extraordinary person when they saw his
compassion and broken heart toward the thirsty and the sick. Yet they had not grasped
the deity of Jesus--who Jesus really is. But when they saw Jesus rebuking the wind and
the sea, they realized that Jesus is God who rules the heavens and the earth. In fear and
amazement they asked one another, “Who is this? He commands even the winds and
the water, and they obey him” (25). For the first time the disciples realized who Jesus
really is.
Jesus had intended to have a retreat with his disciples so that he could explain
more about the secrets of the kingdom of God. But the storm frustrated the plan.
Instead, the storm became an opportunity for Jesus to train them. Jesus wanted to raise
his disciples to be the future leaders for his people. They should be the men who could
handle all difficult situations with courage and by faith in God. But when the sea became
violent, they became fearful. They were overwhelmed by their human situation. Their
fear showed that they had to grow in faith. Fear is inversely related to faith. When faith
is lacking, fear becomes strong. When faith is strong, fear goes away. Fear is planted by
Satan; courage comes from faith in Jesus.
Let us read verse 25a together. “Where is your faith?” he asked his disciples.
Jesus asked them the question, “Where is your faith?” He rebuked them for their lack of
faith. He did not say to them, “Are you okay? That was some storm. I am sorry you had
to go through the terrible storm.” Instead he said, “Where is your faith?” The raging
storm was not the real problem. Their lack of faith was. We must also hear the voice of

Jesus, “Where is your faith?” Do we have faith that Jesus is God who rules the heavens
and nature? Do we have the reverent fear of God and fear nothing else in the world?
We all want to have a smooth sailing in our voyage. But the fact of life is that we
have to face many small and big storms of life. Some people have to face the storm of a
broken family. Some meet the storms of financial difficulty, health problem, job loss,
school problem, and so on. Many college graduates become anxious about their
uncertain future. No one’s life is without storms. In fact, life is about how to overcome
storms.
But we don’t like storms in life. We want our life to be free of troubles. We even
ask the question, “Why do bad things happen to good people?” when we face difficulties
in life. Was it because the disciples had done something wrong that they met the
storms? No. They were acting in obedience to Jesus’ command. In fact, if one of them
had said, “No, I am not going to the other side,” he would have been spared of the storm.
Then why did it happen? Did Jesus know that the storm would come? Absolutely! He is
God. He is sovereign. He didn’t create the storm, but he knew about it. And he allowed
his disciples to face it. Why? The Bible says that God allows his children to face trials in
life so that they may become stronger. The Lord disciplines the one he loves (Heb 12:6).
We must understand that God gives storms of life to those who love him. Sometimes
those who work hard for the Lord expect a pleasant human condition in return. But we
must remember that God disciplines those he loves through the storms of life. The
storms of life are no accident. They are precisely God’s best love to each person.
In order to overcome the storms of life, we must discipline ourselves. Without
discipline no one can be a useful man or woman of faith. Jesus is the best example.
Hebrews 5:8-9 says, “Although he was a son, he learned obedience from what he
suffered and, once made perfect, he became the source of eternal salvation for all who
obey him.” The Son of God had to face the biggest storm in life. It was to suffer and die
on a cross in order to save terrible sinners like you and me. It was to obey God’s will for
world salvation. How much more we need training to obey the Lord! To have faith in
God in the midst of storms is not easy. To help and pray for proud young people is a
hard thing for us to do. But we must know that this is what our Lord Jesus did. To follow
Jesus as his disciples, we must do the same. Jesus didn’t promise an easy life for his
disciples. He said, “Whoever wants to be my disciple must deny themselves and take up
their cross and follow me” (Mk 8:34).
Jesus asked, “Where is your faith?” He wants us to have faith. What does it mean
to have faith in God or to live by faith? It is easy for us to have theoretical faith, but we
know that such faith means very little in overcoming our storms in life. Martha and
Mary had faith in resurrection in theory, but they were inconsolable when their brother
died, even though Jesus came to help them. So what does it mean to have faith? Once
Billy Graham explained faith as he was preaching on the platform built in a football
stadium. He said, “I am standing on this platform built by our carpenters. I have faith
that it is strong to hold me. So I don’t have to be afraid.”

The Golden Gate Bridge in San Francisco was completed in 1937 after 4 years of
difficult construction. The engineers and construction workers had to work on cables
that were hung several hundred feet above the water. If they looked down, they felt
dizzy. Their legs became wobbly. They were paralyzed with the fear of falling to the
water and several of them did fall. Then the city officials decided to install safety nets to
catch anyone who fell. Afterwards, the workers overcame their fear, knowing that there
were safety nets under them. They had faith in the safe nets. Likewise, we Christians
have a safety net in Jesus. We can walk confidently in times of good and bad, knowing
that Jesus will catch us if we fall. We can trust him. This is faith. This is what Jesus
wants us to have when he asked, “Where is your faith?”
When life throws us a curve ball, we can respond in two ways. We can kick and
scream and curse, like many people do. Or we can put our trust in God and come out
stronger by overcoming the trial by faith. Jesus asks each of us, “Where is your faith?”
Jesus wants us to have faith in him as the Lord God. May God help us to have faith in
Jesus our Lord so that we may overcome storms in life and render glory to God!
