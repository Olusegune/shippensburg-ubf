THE GREATEST COMMANDMENT
Mark 12:28-34
Key Verse: 12:29
“The most important one,” answered Jesus, “is this: „Hear, O Israel, the Lord our
God, the Lord is one.‟”
Last week, we learned that our God is the God of the living. Our God is the Ruler
of history who is advancing his salvation work right at this moment. Jesus also taught us
that heaven will be different from this world. There will be no struggle with marriage or
other human matters in heaven, because we will be like the angels.
In today‟s passage, one of the teachers of the law comes to Jesus to ask an
important question about the Bible. He was different from the other religious leaders;
he had a learning mind. May God help us to have a learning mind and find out which is
the greatest commandment of God!
I. LOVE THE LORD YOUR GOD (28-30)
Look at verse 28. One of the teachers of the law came and heard them debating.
Noticing that Jesus had given them a good answer, he asked him, “Of all the
commandments, which is the most important?” Earlier the Pharisees asked Jesus a
loaded question about paying taxes to Caesar to catch him in his words (12:13). Then the
Sadducees came and asked him a question about marriage at the resurrection in an
attempt to undermine his teaching of resurrection (12:18-27). These religious leaders
had a serious spiritual problem. But the teacher in today‟s passage was different. How
was he different? When he heard the debate between the religious leaders and Jesus,
and how Jesus gave them a good answer, this teacher wanted to learn more about the
Scriptures from Jesus. The Pharisees and the Sadducees had come to Jesus with tricky
questions in an attempt to trap him. They had no learning minds. They had no love of
God in their hearts. But this teacher acknowledged the truth taught by Jesus. He loved
the truth. He had a pure heart toward God‟s word. His fellow religious leaders were
preoccupied with maintaining their political status. But this teacher was seeking the
truth. Indeed, it is amazing to see how this man kept his pure heart toward the word of
God in that environment.
How could this teacher keep himself pure in the sight of God? He reminds us of
Psalm 119:9-12: “How can a young man keep his way pure? By living according to your
word. I seek you with all my heart; do not let me stray from your commands. I have
hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you. Praise be to you, O Lord;
teach me your decrees.” As descendants of Adam, sinful men are naturally proud before
God. They want to follow their sinful ways. When they follow their own ways, their
thinking often becomes foolish (Ro 1:22). Do you remember the wicked tenants in the
parable of the vineyard? (12:1-12) They thought that the vineyard would become theirs if
they killed the owner‟s only son. Many people in our society claim that the humanity will
do better without God. But such people‟s thinking often becomes foolish. The only way
we can keep our hearts pure is to study the word of God and obey him. Psalm 119:15-16

says: “I meditate on your precepts and consider your ways. I delight in your decrees; I
will not neglect your word.” This young teacher must have struggled a lot to follow the
word of God against the trend of his generation. Perhaps he even felt lonely. But in
God‟s eyes, he was on a solid ground. He was like a tree planted by the streams of water
(Ps 1:3). Jesus was very happy when this man came to him with a sincere attitude to
learn from him.
We are also greatly encouraged when we find some young students who are eager
to learn spiritual truths through studying the Bible. Many students today are more
interested in things that would satisfy their physical and materialistic desires than the
truth. They would rather go to parties or just hang out than spend time to study the
Bible or have fellowship with other believers. But there are some precious young men
and women who want to know Jesus personally through their sincere Bible studies.
They are truth-seekers. We experience deep joy in our hearts when we study the word of
God with those who have learning minds. We can understand why Jesus was happy to
see this teacher of the law who was willing to hear the word of truth.
Look at verse 28 again. The young teacher asked Jesus, “Of all the
commandments, which is the most important?” This question shows that he was a
sincere Bible student. He asked Jesus one question that points him to the essence of the
Bible. He wanted to know the secret of the Bible. What prompted him to ask this
question?
God gave the Ten Commandments to his people to be a holy nation. But by Jesus‟
time, they had many more additional laws and regulations, and traditions of the elders.
The Sabbath law alone had 39 basic categories of work that shouldn‟t be done. For
example, they were not supposed to walk more than ¾ mile on Sabbath (Ac 1:12). They
were not supposed to carry anything heavier than two dried figs. There were brooches a
woman must not pin to her dress although it was okay if they were stitched to the dress.
They were guilty of harvesting if they plucked heads of grain on Sabbath, guilty of
threshing if they rubbed it in their hands, and guilty of winnowing if they separated
grain from chaff, and so on. We can imagine how people were buried under mountains
of law and regulations in their daily life. It was difficult to see which one was really
important and which one was marginally important. It was hard to meet God personally
through these complicated laws. So the teacher asked Jesus, “Of all the commandments,
which is the most important?”
What was Jesus‟ answer to his sincere question? Look at verse 29. “The most
important one,” answered Jesus, “is this: „Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is
one.‟” We can learn who God is and how we must love him from Jesus‟ answer.
First, “the Lord our God, the Lord is one.” “The most important one,” answered
Jesus, “is this: „Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one.‟” Jesus says that the
most important truth about the Bible is that the Lord our God is one. We must know
that our God is the only true God. The Bible teaches us this truth in many ways, but let‟s
think about only three points today.
First of all, Genesis 1:1 says, “In the beginning God created the heavens and the
earth.” Our God is the Creator God. He is the maker of all things including our lives. We

are here because God put us here for a purpose. He created us in his own image and
blessed us to be fruitful. Our God loved us from the very beginning!
Secondly, our God sent his one and only Son Jesus to save us from our sin and
death. There are many religions in this world. Some people say that it does not matter
which one to believe. This idea appeals to them because it sounds tolerant. But Jesus
teaches us that the Lord our God, the Lord is one. He is “the only true God” (Jn 17:3).
Many other religions are full of illuminations and euphoria but they cannot solve our sin
problem. They claim that we humans can earn heaven or nirvana if we somehow do
enough good. But we know that none of us can be perfectly good all the time. We fail to
be good, time and again. Some people are better than others, but even they cannot be
perfectly good. There is no one righteous, not even one (Ro 3:10). No one can earn
salvation by doing good things. Our God knows this. So he sent his one and only Son
Jesus to die on the cross in order to wash away our sins. Jesus is the gate through which
we can enter the kingdom of God. Our true God opened the way to salvation through his
Son. Jesus is the way and the truth and the life (Jn 14:6). No one comes to God except
through the Son of God.
Thirdly, our God is the only true God who gives us a living hope in the kingdom
of God. There is nothing permanent in this world. Everything will eventually perish,
spoil or fade away. No man can live forever. The human mortality rate is 100 percent.
But our God gives us a living hope that will not perish or spoil. By raising Jesus from the
dead, he opened the way to his eternal kingdom for us. Only our God solved the
fundamental problem of mankind and has given us a true hope through his Son Jesus
Christ.
Let us remember what Jesus said in verse 29. “The most important one is this:
„Hear, O Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one.‟” Let us not turn to worthless gods or
idols of our generation such as money, fame, pleasure, and the like. May God help each
one of us to worship the Lord our God only!
Second, “love the Lord your God with all your heart.” Look at verse 30. “Love
the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind
and with all your strength.” God created us in his own image, so that we may bear
abundant fruit in him. Even when we sinned against him and wandered away from him,
he did not abandon us, but sent his one and only Son Jesus to save us. God is love. We
must love him.
But it is not easy to love God. Many people love their children and their dogs, but
not God. Why? Because God is Spirit who is invisible, we must have a pure heart to see
him and love him. How can we love God? What does it mean to love God? We must first
accept his love towards us. We must experience his amazing love shown to us through
his son Jesus Christ. His love is deep and wide. If we come to know his love, even a
fraction of it, we can love God. But to love God is more than emotional experience. It is
certainly not a lip service. True love requires action. When you truly love someone, you
want to be with the person and do something that pleases the person. That‟s why Jesus
says in John 14:15, “If you love me, you will obey what I command.” The Apostle John
says in 1 John 2:3-6: “We know that we have come to know him if we obey his
commands. The man who says, „I know him,‟ but does not do what he commands is a

We love God because he loved us first (1Jn 4:19). Jesus says we must love God

II. “LOVE YOUR NEIGHBOR AS YOURSELF” (31-34)
Look at verse 31. “The second is this: „Love your neighbor as yourself.‟ There is
no commandment greater than these.” The commandments, “Love God” and “Love
your neighbor,” sum up all the commandments. They are not some recommended
options but commandments for us to obey. These commandments describe the basic
principles of life and keys to our happiness.
“Love your neighbor as yourself.” We usually love ourselves more than others
because of our sinful nature. Our sniffling cold is more serious to us than someone else‟
pneumonia. It is especially difficult to love the unlovely people, such as arrogant and
rebellious people who have no learning minds. How can we love them? One woman said
to her pastor, “I know that God says I must love others and I try. But there is this one
person I can never love even if I try. I don‟t even want to talk to her.” We know that it
seems impossible for us to love people we don‟t like. Still, Jesus says we must love our
neighbor as ourselves. He didn‟t say we should love only those whom we like. This
means that to love others we must overcome our feelings towards people. Love is more
than feelings. We should make a willful decision to obey the word of Christ. We may not
be able to have warm feelings toward everyone, but we can pray for even those whom we
don‟t like and help them when they are in need. We can say encouraging words to them.
We can love others when we accept the love of God who
sent his one and only Son Jesus. We can love others when we
accept the love of Jesus who died on the cross for us. The cross
of Jesus has both the horizontal and vertical bars. The vertical
bar shows our love relationship with God. The horizontal bar
shows our love relationship with each other. The cross of Jesus
shows that loving the unlovely is hard, but if we know the love
of God, we can love them.
“Love your neighbor as yourself.” Then who is our
neighbor? I am glad you asked the question. Actually, this is
the question that another teacher of the law asked Jesus. In
reply, Jesus told the parable of the Good Samaritan (Lk 10:25-37). In this parable, a
man was traveling from Jerusalem to Jericho when he fell into the hands of robbers who
stripped him of his clothes, beat him and left him on the road half dead. Three
characters appeared in this scene. The first one was a priest who looked very dignified.
The priest saw the badly wounded man but pretended that he did not see anything and
passed by on the other side. Next, a Levite came to the place and saw the wounded man.

He also quickly passed by the man, turning his eyes away from him. Finally, a Samaritan
came to the spot. When he saw the man, he took pity on him. He went to him and
bandaged his wounds, pouring oil and wine on him. He put the man on his own donkey
and took him to an inn and took care of him. The next day, he took out two silver coins
and gave them to the innkeeper. “Look after him,” he said, “and when I return I will
reimburse you for any extra expense you may have.” In the course of helping the
wounded man, the Samaritan sacrificed his valuable time and money. After telling the
parable, Jesus asked, “Who is his neighbor?” The expert in the law replied, “The one
who had mercy on him.” Jesus told him, “Go and do likewise” (Lk 10:37).
The Apostle John says in 1 John 4:20-21: “If anyone says, „I love God,‟ yet hates
his brother, he is a liar. For anyone who does not love his brother, whom he has seen,
cannot love God, whom he has not seen. And he has given us this command: Whoever
loves God must also love his brother.”
This week, let us also think about how we can love our neighbor as ourselves.
What can we do to show our love for our neighbor? As we ponder this question, let us
remember that to love someone means more than just doing a human favor for him or
her. We can also love a person by helping him or her spiritually. It is to share the love of
God by encouraging our neighbors to come to God. Who are our neighbors? There are
many young people in our society who need God‟s love. Let us pray for them and help
them with the word of truth so that they may come to Jesus and receive eternal
salvation.
What was the response of the teacher of the law when he heard Jesus‟ teaching on
the essence of the Bible? Look at verses 32-33. “Well said, teacher,” the man replied.
“You are right in saying that God is one and there is no other but him. To love him with
all your heart, with all your understanding and with all your strength, and to love
your neighbor as yourself is more important than all burnt offerings and sacrifices.”
This teacher was happy to hear the words of Jesus. He was happy to have his own his
belief confirmed through Jesus‟ answer. He knew through his personal Bible study that
loving God and loving neighbors were more important than all other religious
ceremonies they practiced. The problem was that his fellow religious leaders were
focused on burnt offerings and sacrifices. He felt lonely trying to walk against the trend
of his society. So he was very happy to hear the truths of God through Jesus‟ teachings.
When Jesus saw the young teacher loved the truth of God so much, he said to
him, “You are not far from the kingdom of God” (34). Jesus blessed him for his sincere
heart toward the word of God. He was very close to the kingdom of God. All he had to do
was to accept Jesus as his personal Savior.
In today‟s passage, we learn that Jesus is pleased when we have pure hearts
toward the truth of God. Jesus was happy to see one teacher of the law with a sincere
heart towards the word of God in the midst of widespread hypocrisy among the religious
leaders. In our society there seems to be many people who crave for many ideas but
reject the truth of God. Yet, just as God reserved the 7,000 people with pure hearts in
Elijah‟s time, there are many truth seekers around us in our generation. We must help
them with the word of God. Most of all, may God help us to love the Lord and love our
neighbors as ourselves!
