JESUS’ TESTIMONY ABOUT HIMSELF
John 5:31-47
Key Verse: 5:36
“I have testimony weightier than that of John. For the very work that the Father
has given me to finish, and which I am doing, testifies that the Father has sent
me.”
In the first part of this chapter, Jesus healed a man who had been an invalid for 38
years. It was a great work of God. But the Jews refused to honor Jesus. Instead, they
persecuted him for breaking the Sabbath law. But Jesus still loved them and gave them
this wonderful promise: “I tell you the truth, whoever hears my word and believes in him
who sent me has eternal life” (5:24). Yet they refused to hear the voice of Jesus. They
hardened their hearts.
In today's passage, Jesus continues to teach them and us why we should hear his
word and believe him as the Son of God. He also teaches us about the consequences of
studying the word of God superficially. May God help us to hear the word of Jesus and
personally believe that Jesus is the Son of God!
I. TESTIMONIES ABOUT JESUS (31-36)
Look at verses 31-32. “If I testify about myself, my testimony is not valid. There is
another who testifies in my favor, and I know that his testimony about me is valid.” The
Jewish leaders did not accept the wonderful promise of Jesus when Jesus said to them, “I
tell you the truth, whoever hears my word and believes in him who sent me has eternal
life and will not be condemned; he has crossed over from death to life” (24). They did not
hear the word of Jesus. Nor did they believe in God who sent Jesus to save them. They
were proud and stubborn. At this point, Jesus could have said, “Fine, they deserve eternal
condemnation. Let them squirm on the Day of Judgment!” But Jesus did not give up on
them. He tried to help them somehow to accept the truth and receive eternal life. How? He
talked to them about testimonies and Moses, which was a familiar topic for the Jewish
leaders. Jesus understood their legalistic mindset. In the Jewish court of law, at least two
witnesses were required to convict or exonerate a person (Dt 19:15; Nu 35:30). So Jesus
said to them: “If I testify about myself, my testimony is not valid.” He was saying, “Okay,
Gentlemen, I know what you are thinking. But my testimony is valid and you should listen
to me, because there are others who testify about me.”
Look at verse 32. “There is another who testifies in my favor, and I know that his
testimony about me is valid.” Jesus reminded the Jews that John the Baptist came as his
witness (1:8). Look at verse 33. “You have sent to John and he has testified to the truth.”
When John’s popularity grew, the Jewish leaders sent a team of investigators to John to
check up on his credentials and to find out what he was up to (Jn 1:19-27). John took the
opportunity to testify to the truth about our Lord Jesus. What was his testimony? John
1:19-20 says, Now this was John’s testimony when the Jews of Jerusalem sent priests and

Levites to ask him who he was. He did not fail to confess, but confessed freely, “I am not
the Christ.” John the Baptist was so popular among his people that many wondered if
John was the Christ, the promised Messiah (Lk 3:15). But he clearly testified, “I am not the
Christ.” He introduced Jesus as the Messiah. When John saw Jesus coming toward him,
he testified: “Look, the Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world!” (1:29) John
1:8 says, “The true light that gives light to every man was coming into the world.” John
testified that Jesus is the true light for all mankind. John testified that Jesus came from
God and is the Son of God (Jn 3:31).
At that time, John was the voice of God for the suffering people. Look at verse 35.
John was a lamp that burned and gave light, and you chose for a time to enjoy his
light. Jesus told them that John was a lamp that burned and gave light for a while. Many
people came to John and heard his message of the baptism of repentance. They responded
to John’s message by repenting their sins with tears (Lk 3:10-14). Tax collectors repented
their sins of selfishness. Prostitutes repented their sins. Even soldiers repented their sins
of extortion and violence. But John’s life of mission was cut short. He gave light to his
people at the cost of his own life. He was beheaded for preaching the truth to King Herod
and his wife. This way, John prepared the way for the Lord Jesus. His testimony was
powerful because he wasn’t afraid of speaking the truth. The Jewish leaders should have
listened to his testimony about Jesus, but they did not.
John the Baptist was a powerful witness to Jesus. But Jesus had even more
powerful witness than John. Look at verse 36. “I have testimony weightier than that of
John. For the very work that the Father has given me to finish, and which I am doing,
testifies that the Father has sent me.” Jesus teaches that the very work that he was doing
was a testimony about him. The work of God that he was doing was more powerful
testimony about him than any person’s words.
God manifested Jesus as his Son by enabling him to perform miracles. At the
beginning of his ministry, Jesus attended a wedding at Cana in Galilee. At the wedding
feast, the wine ran out—a potentially embarrassing situation for the groom’s family. Jesus
blessed the wedding by turning wash water into first class wine (Jn 2:1-11). This event
demonstrated that God sent his Son Jesus to bless his people.
One time, Jesus was traveling through Samaria. He was sitting by the well of Jacob,
tired and hungry, when a lonely and thirsty woman came to draw water. Jesus reached out
to her by humbly asking the question, “Will you give me a drink?” (4:7) Jesus really
wanted to help her to taste the living water welling up to eternal life (4:14). Through his
patient Bible study with her, she accepted Jesus as her personal Savior. She was so happy
to have a new life in Jesus that she left her water jar and went to her people to witness
about Jesus. She was changed from a sinful, despised woman to a powerful witness to
Jesus. Many Samaritans became Christians because of her testimony, “Come, see a man
who told me everything I ever did.” It was a miracle that happened to her life, a great work
of God.

And how about the man who had been an invalid for 38 years? He spent every
waking moments complaining bitterly about his life. Jesus had compassion on this man
and asked him, “Do you want to get well?” Then Jesus commanded him, “Get up! Pick up
your mat and walk” (5:8). For the first time in 38 years, this man could stand on his own
feet.
What happened to these people was a powerful testimony to Jesus that he is the
giver of life. The work of God through Jesus testified that Jesus is the Son of God. But
there is more. The Scriptures also testify that Jesus is the Son of God. God promised to
send the Messiah to save the sinful mankind. The reference to this wonderful promise is
all over the Old Testament scriptures beginning with Genesis 3:15. The prophet Isaiah said
a virgin would give birth to a son and would call him Immanuel—God with us (Isa 7:14).
Isaiah even gave a vivid description of how the Messiah would suffer as the Lamb of God
(Isa 53). Finally, Jesus came to this world as was promised. This was the weightier
testimony than any human words.
Finally and most importantly, God himself is a witness to Jesus. Verse 37 says,
“And the Father who sent me has himself testified concerning me.” From the very
beginning of his Messianic ministry, God was with Jesus. At the moment Jesus was
baptized by John, heaven was opened, and he saw the Spirit of God descending on him like
a dove. And a voice from heaven said, “This is my Son, whom I love; with him I am well
pleased” (Mt 3:16-17). God declared that Jesus is the Savior of the world. If we really
believe the word of God, we accept and believe in Jesus.
II. THE RESULT OF SUPERFICIAL BIBLE STUDY (37-47)
Look at verses 37-40. “And the Father who sent me has himself testified
concerning me. You have never heard his voice nor seen his form, nor does his word
dwell in you, for you do not believe the one he sent. You diligently study the Scriptures
because you think that by them you possess eternal life. These are the Scriptures that
testify about me, yet you refuse to come to me to have life.” God the heavenly Father sent
his one and only Son. And the Son was doing the life-giving work of God. It was God’s
powerful endorsement for his Son. But the Jews refused to believe him. Rather, they
persecuted Jesus and wanted to kill him. Jesus told them that their problem was their
poor attitude toward the Scriptures. They knew very well about the Pentateuch, the five
books of Moses: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers and Deuteronomy. Most of them
memorized these books, word by word.
However, they never heard the voice of God personally through their Bible study.
They studied the Scriptures diligently, but the word of God did not penetrate their hearts.
For instance, the Ten Commandments told them how to love God and how to love their
neighbor. Yet, they had no personal love relationship with God or with their neighbors. It
was because they studied the Bible superficially, only as a means to become a Pharisee or
as a routine in their religious activities. If they had studied the Bible from their hearts, they
would have known God personally and his heart toward his people. They would have

known the Son of God personally and accepted him as their personal Savior, as the
Scriptures testified about Jesus.
This is not just their problem; it is our problem as well. Let us not be mistaken to
think that this is someone else’s problem. It can easily happen to us. It is easy for us to give
more weight to what people say than the word of God. If we study the Bible with a poor
attitude, how can we know the heart of God? How can we love him as he loves us? How
can we have a deep personal relationship with him? We may fall into the serious problem
of the Jewish leaders, that is, looking very religious outwardly but having no love of God in
our hearts. The Jews knew a lot about the Scriptures, but did not even know God’s
testimony about his Son. This is the reason Jesus rebuked them severely: “You diligently
study the Scriptures because you think that by them you possess eternal life. These are
the Scriptures that testify about me, yet you refuse to come to me to have life” (39-40).
A superficial attitude toward the word of God can lead people to all kinds of relative
ideas about God. Some time ago, a local newspaper printed a debate over homosexuality
and abortion. A church minister claimed that homosexuality and abortion are not “real”
sins. He said, “The subject of homosexuality is not mentioned in the Bible. Jesus never
mentioned it, and no prophet talks about it. The word is an invention of the late 19th
century.” These claims are false. Beginning with the book of Genesis, the Bible explicitly
mentions this sin. 1 Corinthians 6:9-10 says: “Do you not know that the wicked will not
inherit the kingdom of God? Do not be deceived: Neither the sexually immoral nor
idolaters nor adulterers nor male prostitutes nor homosexual offenders nor thieves nor
the greedy nor drunkards nor slanderers nor swindlers will inherit the kingdom of God.”
Adultery is a sin. Greed is a sin. Slander is a sin. Sexual immorality is a sin. Likewise,
homosexual offense is a sin. It is not a worse sin than adultery, greed or slander, but it is a
sin. The point is that we must study the Bible as the word of God with awesome respect. It
is easy for us to study the word of God relatively or selectively, accepting only the part we
like, ignoring the others. That’s what the Jewish leaders did. And the result was that they
did not accept the Messiah whom God had sent to save them. They missed the whole point
of the entire Bible!
There was another attitude problem that caused the Jewish leaders not to accept
Jesus as the Messiah. It was their desire to seek people’s praise rather than praise from
God. Look at verse 41. “I do not accept praise from men....” If Jesus had been seeking for
men’s approval, he would have had a friendly relationship with the Jewish leaders. But
Jesus lived for the glory of God. He did not do things to please the Jewish leaders; so he
received many persecutions from them. The Jews were different. They really wanted men’s
praise. Seeking other people’s approval is our problem as well today. We want to be
accepted and validated by other people. This is addicting. So we often ask what others will
think of us rather than what God will think of us. This is what the Jews did. They
meticulously kept all kinds of rituals and traditions in order to impress the people of their
community. They were very religious. But they had no love of God in their hearts (42).
They didn’t love God, nor did they love God’s sheep. They were supposed to be shepherds
for God’s sheep. But they were busy with their own agenda. The invalid man of 38 years
was their sheep. But none of them understood his agony. None of them even tried to help

this man. Instead, they persecuted Jesus for helping him. They made no effort to obtain
the praise that comes from God (44).
Look at verses 45-46. “But do not think I will accuse you before the Father. Your
accuser is Moses, on whom your hopes are set. If you believed Moses, you would believe
me, for he wrote about me. But since you do not believe what he wrote, how are you
going to believe what I say?” The Jews prided themselves for their attachment to Moses,
their great law-giver. So it was an unexpected thrust for Jesus to say that Moses himself
would accuse them before God. They considered Moses as their defense attorney. But
Jesus told them that Moses would be their prosecutor before God. Why? It’s because
Moses wrote about Jesus and they refused to accept Jesus as the Messiah. Even the books
of Moses pointed to the coming Messiah, that is, our Lord Jesus. Genesis 3:15 says that
“the woman’s offspring” will crush the head of Satan. Exodus 12:21 refers to the “Passover
lamb.” Leviticus 16:5 mentions a ram for burnt offering, symbolizing the payment of the
price of Christ’s atonement, while Numbers 24:17 mentions a “scepter” that will rise out of
Israel. Deuteronomy 18:15 says: “The Lord your God will raise up for you a prophet like
me from among your own brothers. You must listen to him.” Although these books did not
mention Jesus by name, they pointed to the coming Messiah. So Jesus told them, “If you
believed Moses, you would believe me, for he wrote about me.”
In conclusion, we learned that there are many witnesses that testify for Jesus as the
giver of eternal life. John the Baptist was one. God himself is the most important witness.
The very work of God Jesus was doing testified on his behalf. Today, the word of God is a
powerful testimony to us about Jesus. We must study the Bible sincerely from our hearts
so that we may know the Son of God personally. May God help us also to be faithful
witnesses of Jesus to young people today!
