JESUS PREDICTS HIS DEATH AGAIN
Luke 9:37-45
Key Verse: 9:44
“Listen carefully to what I am about to tell you: The Son of Man is going to be
delivered into the hands of men.”
Let me begin by asking you a question. What do you think is the most serious
problem facing our generation today? Nuclear threat? Terrorism? Racial disharmony?
Or the nasty personal attacks and bullying on the social media? In today’s passage,
Jesus heals a boy who was tortured by an evil spirit. In doing so, Jesus laments over the
unbelief of the entire generation. Then he predicts his upcoming death to his disciples
again. Let’s listen carefully to what Jesus says to us.
First, a father comes to Jesus with an urgent plea (37-40).
Look at verse 37. “The next day, when they came down from the mountain, a
large crowd met him.” Jesus came down from the mountain of transfiguration with
Peter, James and John. He had been away from the crowd for only one day. Yet they
already missed him. Indeed, without Jesus, they were like sheep without a shepherd.
Jesus was such a good shepherd. He healed many sick people, drove out evil spirits and
preached the good news of the kingdom of God. They wanted to be with Jesus all the
time. As soon as they saw Jesus coming down from the mountain, they were so happy
and ran to meet him. A man in the crowd came forward to Jesus to make an urgent
request for his son.
Look at verses 38-39. A man in the crowd called out, “Teacher, I beg you to look
at my son, for he is my only child. A spirit seizes him and he suddenly screams; it
throws him into convulsions so that he foams at the mouth. It scarcely ever leaves him
and is destroying him.” A father’s joy is to watch his son grow. Even after a hard day at
work, he goes to watch his son playing soccer. He is happy to attend his son’s school
concert even when the orchestra isn’t that good. He is especially happy when his son
brings home a good report card.
However, this father had more urgent matters on his mind than soccer game or
report card. He was desperate. His only son was suffering from demon possession.
When the demon came upon the boy, it “seized” him. The boy did not do what he
wanted to do; he did what the demon told him to do. It was an ongoing nightmare for
him. He never knew when the demon would seize him. He only screamed when it
happened. The demon caused him to have convulsions: his muscles contracted, his face
contorted, and he rolled on the ground, foaming at the mouth. It was an unbearable
thing for any father to watch. In my middle school, a girl student had an epileptic
seizure during a class. It was scary to watch when she foamed at the mouth, because
most of us didn’t know what’s going on. I cannot imagine how she felt afterwards.

2
The boy’s problem here was demon possession. We don’t know how this tragedy
happened to the family. His father must have wanted him to study hard in school and
become a man of good influence. Maybe his father made him a nice study room, with a
book-case full of classics and his own computer. But suddenly one day his son began to
act strangely. He was no longer the sweet son he knew. One moment he was angry,
rebellious and destructive. The next moment he seemed lonely and helpless. The father
had a spiritual insight to see that his son was possessed by an evil spirit. He didn’t know
how his son came to be possessed by a demon. Perhaps one day the boy visited a bad
website. Perhaps it was a satanic videogame he enjoyed playing that opened a door of
his heart for demon. Whatever happened, the boy was now tormented by the evil spirit.
He didn’t want to be controlled by the demon. He hated what the demon made him do.
He wanted to be normal like other boys. But he was helpless. In his helplessness, the boy
would cry out for help. His father wanted to help, but he was also helpless. Who could
possibly help such helpless father and son?
One day the father heard about Jesus of Nazareth. He heard how Jesus drove out
demons from people. He brought his son to Jesus in the hope that he would also heal his
son. But Jesus was not there. He had gone up on the mountain with three disciples. So
the father begged the remaining disciples to drive out the demon (40). The nine
disciples were excited about this opportunity to show what they could do. After all, they
had done such work while they were out on their training mission earlier. They were
also intensely jealous of the three disciples whom Jesus took to the mountain, and
wanted to show they were capable. But when they said to the evil spirit, “Come out of the
boy, you evil spirit,” nothing happened. In their embarrassment, they repeated the
command again and again, but there was no response. It seemed that the evil spirit
began to laugh at them. It was a humiliating situation. The disciples joined the father
and son for their helplessness.
Second, “You unbelieving generation” (41-43a).
When Jesus heard the story, what was his reaction? Look at verse 41. “You
unbelieving and perverse generation,” Jesus replied, “how long shall I stay with you
and put up with you? Bring your son here.” Jesus rebuked the entire generation for
their unbelief. He didn’t say, “I am sorry your son is suffering so much.” He didn’t say, “I
feel your pain.” Instead, Jesus said, “You unbelieving and perverse generation.” Jesus
saw unbelief as their main problem. In Mark’s gospel, we find some details that help us
understand this. The boy’s father did not firmly believe that Jesus could heal his son. He
said tentatively, “...if you can do anything, take pity on us and help us.” “If you can”?
said Jesus. “Everything is possible for one who believes” (Mk 9:23). The disciples had
tried to heal the boy depending on their past experience. They did not pray. They did not
acknowledge that the power to drive out demons comes from God. Jesus told them,
“This kind can come out only by prayer” (Mk 9:29). In addition, the nine disciples were
full of human thinking, because Jesus had not taken them to the mountain. They did not
pray because their hearts were preoccupied with competition, jealousy, and complaint.
In other words, they failed to help the boy because they had no faith.

3
We should know that, more than anything, unbelief causes sorrow for our Lord
Jesus. When Martha and Mary complained bitterly to him for not coming quickly to heal
their brother, Jesus tried to plant resurrection faith in them by saying, “I am the
resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will live, even though he dies; and
whoever lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?” But they had no
real faith. Jesus was deeply troubled by their unbelief. He wept (Jn 11:35). Once Jesus
and his disciples were in the middle of the Sea of Galilee when a furious storm came up
and threatened to capsize their boat. They were afraid of drowning even though Jesus
was right there with them in the boat. Jesus got up and rebuked the wind. The storm
died down immediately. Then he rebuked his disciples, “Why are you so afraid? Do you
still have no faith?” (Mk 4:40).
On the other hand, our faith makes Jesus most happy. Once a Gentile woman
from the region of Syria came to Jesus and begged him to drive out a demon from her
little daughter. Jesus first tested her faith by saying something really harsh. He said,
“First let the children eat all they want, for it is not right to take the children's bread and
toss it to their dogs” (Mk 7:27). Jesus called Jews “children” and her people (Gentiles)
“dogs.” It was an insult to her people. Amazingly, her faith in Jesus didn’t waver. “Yes,
Lord,” she replied, “but even the dogs under the table eat the children’s crumbs.” She
believed in the power and compassion of Jesus that she knew even crumbs of his
blessing would be enough to help her daughter. Jesus was deeply impressed by her faith
and he was very happy. He said to her, “For such a reply, you may go; the demon has left
your daughter.”
Indeed, it is such a blessing to live by faith in God. There was an ordinary couple
with children who lived a happy life. They were Christians but their faith was lukewarm
at best. They were part of unbelieving generation. They did not pay careful attention to
God’s word. Then one day, the husband was diagnosed with lung cancer. He was given
only six months to live. The family was devastated. To their credit, they began to pray to
God sincerely and humbly, repenting their sins. In a time of trial, they turned to God.
They dedicated their lives to the Lord. Strangely, however, the husband’s condition did
not get worse, even after several months. It turned out that he was misdiagnosed. He
didn’t have cancer. Still, the family’s spiritual turnaround was for real. They had used
their trial to turn to God and grow in faith. Their lives were changed for good.
Like the people in this passage, we live among unbelieving and perverse
generation. Demons go around looking for victims to devour. They make us worship
money and pleasure instead of God. We become helpless when we are faced with
hardship, instead of turning to God. We make Jesus sad whenever we are unbelieving
and full of human thinking. Jesus would rebuke us saying, “You unbelieving
generation.” Jesus had a broken heart for them then, and he has a broken heart for us
now. May God help us to repent our unbelief and live by faith!
After lamenting over the unbelief of the generation, Jesus told the father to bring
his son to him. Even while the boy was coming, the demon threw him to the ground in a
convulsion. Demons never give up their victims easily. But Jesus rebuked the evil spirit,
healed the boy and gave him back to his father (42). It was wonderful work of God. The

demon could torture the boy no more. To the father, to see his son happy and healthy
was the best thing that could ever happen. This father must have wept with joy and
gratitude. Jesus saved his son and gave him back to him. The crowd witnessed this
event. They were helpless before the demon. But Jesus drove out the demon. And they
were all amazed at the greatness of God (43). We must have faith in Jesus—that he can
heal any person no matter how serious his or her situation may be.
What else can we learn from Jesus who healed the boy with an evil spirit? Satan
tries very hard to plant doubt and negative thinking in our hearts. When we try to help
our Bible students by faith, sometimes they seem to become worse. Using his clever
schemes, Satan tries to hamper our efforts to live by faith. What should be our attitude
when this happens? We learn that we should rebuke the evil spirit in the name of Jesus
and obey the word of God more faithfully.
Third, Jesus predicts his death a second time (43b-45).
The people were marveling at what Jesus did. The three disciples must have said
to the other disciples, “You guys are very fortunate that we came back.” The nine
disciples had to swallow their humiliation, but they were happy that Jesus had healed
the poor boy. Jesus’ popularity reached another all-time high. The disciples felt that
days of prosperity and success were ahead of them. They looked forward to traveling
with Jesus and doing great work of God among many people. They began to sing,
“Happy days are here again....”
In the midst of the celebration, Jesus took his disciples aside and spoke to them
personally. Let’s read verse 44. “Listen carefully to what I am about to tell you: The Son
of Man is going to be delivered into the hands of men.” He told them he would “be
betrayed into the hands of his enemies” (NLT). Why did Jesus pop their balloons when
they tried to celebrate his popularity? On the mountain of transfiguration, God said to
the disciples, “Listen to him.” Now Jesus told them to listen to him carefully. It was
because they were full of their own ideas and would not listen to Jesus.
Look at verse 44 again. “Listen carefully to what I am about to tell you: The Son
of Man is going to be delivered into the hands of men.” This was a second time Jesus
told them about his suffering and death. This time, however, he did not talk in detail
about his death and resurrection, as he had previously (9:21). He simply told them about
his betrayal. He wanted them to know that betrayal would be the first thing that would
happen to him. One of his own disciples, Judas Iscariot, would betray him, selling him
for 30 silver coins (Mt 26:15). Betrayal is the most difficult pain for any human being to
endure, because betrayal is not done by enemy; betrayal is done by a loved one. Betrayal
is possible where there is love and trust. Jesus, the holy Son of God, was going to be
betrayed by his own disciple whom he loved so much.
Three years earlier, Jesus had called the twelve ordinary men to be his disciples.
He loved them dearly and trusted them. He knew the weaknesses of each one. He knew
Peter had a big mouth. He knew Judas loved money. Yet he loved and trusted them
unconditionally. Jesus even knew he would be betrayed by Judas and abandoned by all

of them. Yet he continued to love and trust them to the end (Jn 13:1). Jesus was willing
to bear the pain of betrayal and even to die on the cross for their sins and ours.
It was time for them to listen carefully to his words. It was time for them to open
their spiritual eyes and see what Jesus was trying to do. It was time to renew their
personal commitment to Jesus. How did they respond? Verse 45 says, “But they did not
understand what this meant. It was hidden from them, so that they did not grasp it,
and they were afraid to ask him about it.” It was hidden from them because they were
unwilling to accept Jesus’ suffering and death. They were afraid to ask him about it
because they didn’t want him to die. Ultimately, their fear came from the fear of death.
They didn’t want to give up their earthly dreams. Jesus knew it would be hard for them
to accept the painful truth. But Jesus told them in advance for their own sake.
Jesus wanted his disciples to examine their hearts on the way to Jerusalem and
accept his suffering, death and resurrection. Jesus wanted them to make personal
decisions of faith to commit their lives to him. A decision of faith is an act of the will to
choose to obey God even when it involves suffering. A decision of faith can be made only
through much struggle in prayer. What Jesus’ disciples really needed was to make a
decision of faith on the road to Jerusalem.
Likewise, we really need to make decisions of faith to follow Jesus. We can follow
Jesus as part of the crowd for a while. We can enjoy the spiritual atmosphere of Jesus’
ministry and the wonderful work of God as spectators for a while. But we would remain
as part of unbelieving generation if we continue that. Each of us must make a personal
decision of faith in order to follow Jesus. Jesus said, “Whoever wants to be my disciple
must deny themselves and take up their cross daily and follow me” (9:23).
In today’s passage, we learn that we are unbelieving and perverse generation. We
must repent our unbelief and listen to Jesus by faith. Jesus was betrayed for our sake.
He was crucified in order to save us from our sin and death. May God help us to make
personal decisions of faith to follow Jesus to the end!
