JESUS IS THE WAY
John 14:1-14
Key Verse: 14:6
Jesus answered, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the
Father except through me.”
In today’s passage, Jesus declares that he is the way and the truth and the life.
Jesus is the way to heaven. To know the way is to have a clear direction and purpose in
life. May God help us to know what it means that Jesus is the way, the truth and the life!
I. “MY FATHER’S HOUSE” (1-4)
Look at verse 1. “Do not let your hearts be troubled. You believe in God; believe
also in me.” Jesus saw the troubled faces of his disciples. They had an anxiety attack.
Why were they so anxious? During the Last Supper, Jesus washed their feet. Then he
shocked them by talking about his betrayal by one of the Twelve. He also said, “My
children, I will be with you only a little longer… Where I am going, you cannot come”
(13:33). Peter did not like what he was hearing. He asked, “Lord, why can’t I follow you
now? I will lay down my life for you.” Then Jesus said that Peter would disown him
three times that very night (13:38). This was too much for them to take. They were
overwhelmed with all kinds of anxiety. They had left everything to follow Jesus, hoping
for a better life. For three and a half years, Jesus took care of them, traveling with them
everywhere, teaching them the word of God, and training them to be men of faith. They
became excited when his popularity grew rapidly. Each time Jesus performed a miracle,
they felt good about following him as his disciples. They were dreaming about getting
cabinet positions in the earthly messianic kingdom they thought Jesus would establish.
But now their dreams were about to be shattered into pieces. They felt like campaign
workers whose candidate just lost an election. They were weighed down with anxiety
about their uncertain future. What would they do without Jesus? They had no idea
about what to do and where to go without their master. They became restless. They were
like people in our generation who are gripped with anxieties because of uncertain future.
How did Jesus help them? Jesus did not say sympathetic things like, “I am sorry I
have to go,” or “Take care of yourselves.” Instead, he said, “Do not let your hearts be
troubled. You believe in God; believe also in me.” Jesus knew that their problem was not
their human situation or future uncertainty. It was lack of their faith in God. So he told
them to have faith in God. Faith in God overcomes the world. What does it mean to
believe in God and believe in Jesus? To believe in God means to accept God’s word of
promise in our hearts. Matthew 6:33 is just one of many promises of Jesus. Many men
and women of God in history overcame their anxieties by holding on to this promise:
“Seek first his kingdom and his righteousness, and all these things will be given to you as
well.” Jesus saw that his disciples’ hearts were troubled. What glorious promise did
Jesus give them to help them?

Look at verses 2-4. “My Father’s house has many rooms; if that were not so,
would I have told you that I am going there to prepare a place for you? And if I go and
prepare a place for you, I will come back and take you to be with me that you also may
be where I am. You know the way to the place where I am going.” In these verses Jesus
promises a place in heaven for each of us. The kingdom of God is the ultimate hope for
all Christians. This is the key point of the gospel. Jesus died on the cross and rose from
the dead in order to fulfill this promise of the kingdom of God. His cross and
resurrection opened the door to the kingdom for those who believe in him, including you
and me.
Notice that Jesus called the kingdom of God “my Father’s house.” Why? It was
because the disciples had only a vague idea about the kingdom of God. Their dreams and
expectations were earth-bound. The kingdom of God was not real to them. Sadly, this is
true to some Christians today as well. Let us read verse 2 together. “My Father’s house
has many rooms; if that were not so, would I have told you that I am going there to
prepare a place for you?” Jesus teaches us that heaven is real--as real as our father’s
house. If I asked you what heaven is, how would you answer? Many of us will say that
heaven is where God is. And that is true. The problem is that heaven seems unreal, like
an imaginary place that is somewhere over the rainbow. We tend to imagine heaven as a
far-away place where God and angels floating around on white clouds. But Jesus says
clearly, “My Father’s house has many rooms.” At the end of each semester, our students
say, “I am going home,” meaning they are going to their parents’ house. When you say,
“my parents’ house,” you mean a real house, one with red bricks or white sidings, which
has bedrooms, living room and kitchen with delicious smell of food. Your parents’ house
is real. But, somehow, heaven seems to be different. It is a far-away place that does not
seem real. In this passage, Jesus teaches us that heaven is real. It is as real as our
parents’ house. He says there are many rooms in our Father’s house. And he was going
there to prepare a place for us. It is not an imaginary place. It is a real place. Then he
gives us this promise: And if I go and prepare a place for you, I will come back and
take you to be with me that you also may be where I am.
Throughout history, our God has been faithful in keeping his promises. He kept
his promises to Adam and Eve, and Noah and his family. He kept his promises to Moses
and the Israelites. He kept his promises to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, to Zechariah and
Elizabeth, and to Mary. Our God has a good track record of keeping his words. We can
trust in God because he has never reneged on his promises. And the promise of the
kingdom of God is the ultimate promise. This is the main point of the gospel. Jesus’ very
first message was “The kingdom of God is near. Repent and believe the good news!” (Mk
1:15) Jesus planted this hope of the kingdom of God throughout his ministry. Why?
Because the kingdom of God is our true hope. Man cannot live without hope. But there
is no everlasting hope in this passing world. All hopes based on this world will eventually
disappoint us. The kingdom of God is the only everlasting hope for all mankind (2Co
5:1). May God help us to accept Jesus’ promise of the kingdom of God!

II. “I AM THE WAY AND THE TRUTH AND THE LIFE” (5-7)
Jesus’ promise of the kingdom of God did not touch the disciples’ hearts. Because
they were still young and they liked this world. Likewise, we tend to make the same
mistake that the kingdom of God is something only elderly people should think about.
In response to Jesus’ glorious promise, Thomas said to him, “Lord, we don’t
know where you are going, so how can we know the way?” (5) Jesus was very clear on
where he was going. He was going to his Father’s house to prepare a place for us. But
Thomas didn’t get it. He said he still did not know where Jesus was going. He felt he was
lost. He was lost because he did not accept the words of Jesus. Jesus had told them
where he came from and where he was going. But Thomas insisted on his own logic and
human way of thinking. He could not accept the glorious promise of Jesus.
To this doubting man, Jesus gave one of the most important verses in the Bible.
Let us read verse 6. Jesus answered, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one
comes to the Father except through me.” No one ever dared to declare, “I am the way
and the truth and the life.” But Jesus declares in this verse that he is the way and the
truth and the life. Then what does it mean that Jesus is the way and the truth and the
life?
First, Jesus is the way. Jesus is the way to heaven. Why is Jesus the way? Jesus is the
way because he opened the way to the kingdom of God through his death on the cross
and resurrection. Before Jesus, men could not approach the righteous God because of
their sins. But Jesus set us free from the bondage of sin and death through his cross and
resurrection. Jesus made it possible for us to gain access to the righteous God (Ro 5:1-2).
Jesus is the way of salvation. Jesus is the only way to the kingdom of God. Let us
memorize verse 6. Jesus answered, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one
comes to the Father except through me.” In our pluralistic society, people cringe at our
belief that Jesus is the only way to heaven. They would call us narrow-minded bigots.
But Jesus is not a way to heaven. He is the way. He is the only way. No one can come to
God except through Jesus. It is because he is the only one who gave his life on the cross
to take away our sins. He is the only one who rose from the dead on the third day to
open the door to the Kingdom of God.
When I decided to follow Jesus as a lay missionary for campus mission, one of my
college professors, who cared for me dearly, was not happy. He advised me to reconsider
it. He told me to climb all mountains first before making a decision like that. To his eyes,
my decision to become a missionary was based on the inexperience of a young man. But
in fact, God helped me to make the decision based on Mark 1:17. I thank God who has
blessed my decision and guided me to this day. I was a wretched sinner who was lost in
the darkness of sin. But Jesus shined his light on me and gave me a true purpose and life
direction in campus mission. Before Jesus came into my life, I only had small hopes for
life, such as a pretty house with garden, a job with good pay, and so on, not knowing that
these hopes would disappoint me in the end. Now I have the hope of the kingdom of God

that will never disappoint me! Indeed there are many ways in this world. That is the
reason many people are lost. But Jesus is the only way to the kingdom of God.
Second, Jesus is the truth. What is truth? Truth is what is true in all situations.
Truth remains true when all other things change over time. Truth remains constant even
when circumstances change. Truth is also universal, applying to everyone in the world; it
must be true to all people, rich or poor, wise or foolish, strong or weak. Where is such
thing in this world? Nothing in this world is permanent. Things of this world perish,
spoil and fade away (1Pe 1:4). Therefore, truth must come from God who is eternal.
Jesus is the truth because he came from God. Jesus is the truth because he is the same
yesterday, today and tomorrow (Heb 13:8).
John 17:17 says, “Sanctify them by the truth; your word is truth.” The word of
God is truth because it never changes. 1 Peter 1:24-25 read: “For, ‘All men are like grass,
and all their glory is like the flowers of the field; the grass withers and the flowers fall,
but the word of the Lord stands forever.’“ The word of the Lord never changes; as such
the promise of the kingdom of God never changes. Heaven and earth will change, but
the word of God will never change. John 8:31-32 says: To the Jews who had believed
him, Jesus said, “If you hold to my teaching, you are really my disciples. Then you will
know the truth, and the truth will set you free.” The word of Jesus can set us free from
the bondage of sin and death!
Third, Jesus is the life. Jesus is the life because he is the Creator God. John 1:1-4
says: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was
God. He was with God in the beginning. Through him all things were made; without
him nothing was made that has been made. In him was life, and that life was the light
of men. Jesus is the life because he is the author of life, and thus the source of life. He
gives eternal life to whoever comes to him and believes in him (3:16; 5:24). You might
say that you already have a life. There are two kinds of life, physical life and eternal life.
Today, people are living with material abundance that their ancestors did not have. People
can enjoy bits and pieces of comfort and convenience. So they should be happier than their
ancestors. Strangely, there are many unhappy people. Many people don’t have life
because they don’t let Jesus come into their lives. They may look good or even beautiful
outwardly, but they don’t have life in them, just like cut flowers in a vase. What is the
most valuable thing to a person? Of course, it is his or her life. It is his or her spiritual
life or soul because our physical life is rather limited. So Jesus says in Mark 8:36-37:
“What good is it for a man to gain the whole world, yet forfeit his soul? Or what can a
man give in exchange for his soul?” Jesus gives eternal life to those who come to him.
Jesus is waiting at the door of our hearts. May God help us to accept Jesus so that we
may have eternal life and the true hope for the kingdom of God!
John 5:24 says: “I tell you the truth, whoever hears my word and believes him
who sent me has eternal life and will not be condemned; he has crossed over from death
to life.” This verse is a wonderful promise of God for all sinners like you and me. This verse
gives hope to all those who are condemned because of their sins. Jesus promises us eternal

life. This is the reason why he came to this world, to serve sinners and to die for them on
the cross.
Again, how can we receive eternal life? Hearing the word of God is a first step.
Jesus promises that those who hear the word of God and believe him have eternal life. The
way of salvation is very simple. But some people are engrossed with their sinful desires.
They want to hear only what they want to hear. They hear the word of God, but only
selectively. The Jews heard the word of God, but because they were full of themselves, it

believe Jesus who was sent by God to save us from our sins. Then God gives us eternal life.
We have crossed over from death to life!
We don’t know what will happen to our life tomorrow. We don’t know when God
will call us home. But we do know that when our life is done in this world, we will be with
Jesus in the kingdom of God. Do you have this assurance?
III. “WHATEVER YOU ASK IN MY NAME” (8-14)
Look at verse 8. Philip said, “Lord, show us the Father and that will be enough
for us.” Philip did not believe in the kingdom of God because he did not see God with his
human eyes. Perhaps his motto was, “I don’t see, therefore I don’t believe.” There are
many Philips in this world. If they don’t see any immediate benefits, they refuse to
believe. To them, the kingdom of God is no more than a fairy tale.
How did Jesus help Philip? Look at verse 9. Jesus answered: “Don’t you know
me, Philip, even after I have been among you such a long time? Anyone who has seen
me has seen the Father. How can you say, ‘Show us the Father’?” In fact, Philip had
seen God in Jesus (1:18). He had also seen the miraculous signs through which Jesus
had demonstrated the mighty power of God (11). In the face of the overwhelming
evidence, why didn’t Philip believe? It was because he calculated. As we studied in
Chapter 6, Philip had a math-oriented mind. At a glance he could estimate the size of the
crowd of over 5,000 and calculate how much food would be needed to give each person a
bite. When he calculated, everything looked negative. Even Jesus’ word of promise
didn’t seem to add up.
Jesus still loved Philip to the end. Look at verse 12. “Very truly I tell you,
whoever believes in me will do the works I have been doing, and they will do even
greater things than these, because I am going to the Father.” Jesus encouraged Philip
to believe rather than calculate. Jesus gave him a vision to become a great man who can
do great things by faith in God.
How could Philip do greater things than Jesus? Look at verses 13-14. “And I will
do whatever you ask in my name, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. You
may ask me for anything in my name, and I will do it.” This tells us that one who prays
can do great work of God. Usually smart people calculate rather than pray. They depend

on their own ability rather than the Almighty God. They are too proud to pray to God.
The problem is that when things don’t go according to their calculation, they give up and
despair. Philip had this problem. So Jesus taught him the power of prayer.
When the Apostle John was young, he was ambitious and confident. He did not
value the power of prayer. So he did not pray. He tried to catch some sleep even when
Jesus struggled in prayer. But later, he learned that prayer was the only source of his
strength. He said, “This is the confidence we have in approaching God: that if we ask
anything according to his will, he hears us” (1Jn 5:14). Jesus wants all of us to trust in
God in prayer. Jesus wants us to do great work by depending on God in prayer.
In this passage, we learned that Jesus is the way to heaven. Without hope in the
kingdom of God, we cannot but become anxious in this age of anxiety. But Jesus assures
us that he has gone to the Father to prepare a place for us. May God help us to do great
things through prayer while we wait for his Second Coming to take us back to the
Father’s house!
