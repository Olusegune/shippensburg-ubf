Open Wide Your Hearts

2 Corinthians 6:3-7:16
Key Verse: 6:13

PAUL APPEALS FOR RECONCILIATION (6:3-7:1)

1. What was Paul's purpose in commending himself as God's servant? (6:3-4a)
What is the first thing he mentioned? What were the things he endured?
(6:4b-5) How could he have such a great endurance? (4:17-18; Heb 12:1-2)

2. How did Paul describe his inner spiritual life? (6:6-7) What was its
source? How did people respond to Paul and how did he maintain his
identity as God's servant? (6:8-9a) What were the paradoxical contrasts
in his life? (6:9b-10) What do you think was the secret of his life of
victory and blessing? (4:14; Ro 8:37)

3. How had Paul opened himself up to the Corinthians? (6:11-12) What kind
of relationship did he have with them? (6:13a) How did he want them to
respond? (6:13b)

4. What is Paul's directive for the Corinthian believers? (6:14a) What
characterizes unbelievers? (6:14b-16a) Why can there be no fellowship
between Christ's followers and idol-worshipers? (cf. 1Co 5:9-11)

5. To enjoy the privilege of God's dwelling among us, what attitude must we
have? (6:16b-17) When we do so, what relationship does God make with us?
(6:18) In light of these promises, what should we do? (7:1)

PAUL REJOICES OVER THE CHURCH'S REPENTANCE (7:2-16)

6. What appeal did Paul make to the Corinthians, and on what basis? (7:2-4)
How does he describe his affection for and confidence in them? Why did
they need to know this?

7. How did Paul describe his agony while in Macedonia? (7:5) How did God
comfort him, especially through their restored relationship? (7:6-7)

8. How had Paul's letter affected them? (7:8-9) What contrast does Paul
make between godly and worldly sorrow? (7:10) What does worldly sorrow
bring? (Mt 27:3-5) What were the fruits of godly sorrow in them? (7:11-
13a; cf. Lk 22:61-62) In light of this, how can we help others to be
reconciled with God and with his people?

9. What role did Titus play in helping the Corinthian church? (7:13b-16)
Why was Paul so happy that they had proved themselves to him?
