JESUS’ HIGH PRIESTLY PRAYER
John 17:1-26
Key Verse: 17:1
After Jesus said this, he looked toward heaven and prayed: “Father, the hour
has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you.”
Suppose you expect that something horrible will happen to you in the next few
hours. What would be your prayer topics? Today’s passage records Jesus’ high priestly
prayer. Just before his crucifixion, Jesus prayed for himself, for his disciples, and for all
believers. His prayer reveals what was really on his mind as he braced for his upcoming
suffering and death. May the Holy Spirit teach us how much Jesus loves us as we study
his prayer!
I. JESUS PRAYS FOR HIMSELF (1-5)
Look at verse 1. After Jesus said this, he looked toward heaven and prayed:
“Father, the hour has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you.” In
John’s gospel, the words “my hour” or “my time” are repeated, indicating Jesus followed
God’s schedule for him. Now “the hour” had come for him to die on the cross as the
Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world (1:29). Crucifixion was the most
inhumane way to execute a person. The state supreme court of Georgia banned the
electric chair, citing its cruelty. But crucifixion was much more inhumane, not only
physically but also mentally. The victim’s naked body was hung publicly for his family
and others to see. His hands and feet were nailed to the cross. Death would not come
quickly, sometimes not until days later. After death, his body was left to rot or eaten by
scavengers. Jesus is the Son of God. He was the only person without sin on earth. But he
was about to be crucified for the sins that you and I committed. He prayed, “Father, the
hour has come. Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you.” He prayed that God
would glorify him so that he might glorify God. What does this mean? The word “glorify”
(doxazo () in Greek) means “to give honor” or “to reveal.” Jesus wanted to reveal
God’s glory through his death on the cross.
Jesus’ prayer revealed the purpose in his life. It was to glorify God by obeying
God’s will. “Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you.” This prayer teaches us
what our purpose or goal in life should be. What is your goal in life? I often ask the
question to my students in my classes. Most of them would answer: “It is to make a lot
of money and to be happy.” They do not know that God created each of us for his glory.
The Bible teaches us that man’s chief end is to glorify God and enjoy him forever (Ro
11:36). To live to gratify our sinful nature was not what God intended for our life.
Before Jesus saved me, I lived a selfish life following my sinful nature. As a result,
I was a burden to myself and to others. I was not a blessing. I did not have a clear
purpose in life. I was like a dead fish flowing down the stream. But since Jesus came into
my life, my goal in life was changed. I know now and believe that God created me in his
own image, not to live a selfish life, but to live a life that glorifies him. Today, I see many

Before his conversion, the apostle Paul was an ambitious young man. He lived his
life in his own way. But after he met the risen Christ he came to know the truth about

prayer in this passage. Jesus wanted to glorify God even through his suffering and death.
He wanted to glorify God by completing his mission as the Lamb of God. May God help
us to remember this prayer so that we may live for the glory of God despite difficulties in
our life of mission!
Look at verse 2. “For you granted him authority over all people that he might
give eternal life to all those you have given him.” The hour had come for him to die on
the cross. But Jesus did not despair. He knew that God granted him authority to give the
gift of eternal life to all believers. His death would be followed by his glorious
resurrection. He would conquer sin and death. He would open the door to the kingdom
of heaven so that we may be with him forever.
People pay little attention to eternal life. In the hustle and bustle of the modern
day life with smartphones and social media, there is no time for them to stop to think
about life. Many young people consider eternal life as something they can worry about
later when they get old. In addition, many people have a wrong concept of eternal life.
Contrary to their notion, eternal life is not an extension of life in this world. It is not an
upgrade of this imperfect world. Jesus says in verse 3: “Now this is eternal life: that they
know you, the only true God, and Jesus Christ, whom you have sent.” What does it
mean to know God and Jesus Christ? To know God is much more than to have mere
intellectual knowledge of him. It is to have a personal relationship with him. To know
Jesus is to love him and to obey his words (Jn 14:23). When we come to know him
through a personal relationship, eternal life begins in us even in this world. Of course,
we will have eternal life in the kingdom of God that is yet to come.
Some people consider Hawaii as a paradise. Its pristine beaches and beautiful
flowers are indeed remarkable to see. But it’s no heaven. In fact, a paradise is no
paradise if Jesus is not there. Heaven is heaven because we will be with Jesus there. Do
you want to live in heaven just because its streets are paved with gold? No. We want to
be in heaven, because Jesus, who loves us so much, will be there with us forever.
Jesus rendered glory to God while on earth by completing the work that God gave
him to do (4). His mission was to plant in people’s hearts the love of God, faith and hope
of the kingdom of God. It was not easy. He suffered a lot. Through his suffering, Jesus
glorified God. Look at verse 5. “And now, Father, glorify me in your presence with the
glory I had with you before the world began.” The lost paradise and its restoration are

II. JESUS PRAYS FOR HIS DISCIPLES (6-19)
Look at verses 6-8. “I have revealed you to those whom you gave me out of the
world. They were yours; you gave them to me and they have obeyed your word. Now
they know that everything you have given me comes from you. For I gave them the
words you gave me and they accepted them. They knew with certainty that I came
from you, and they believed that you sent me.” The twelve disciples were ordinary men.
They were not exceptionally bright. Neither were they exceptionally faithful. Still, Jesus
loved them very much because God gave them to him. So what did he do for them
during his time on earth? He revealed God to them. How? He taught them the words of
God and they accepted them. Through the word of God, the disciples believed that Jesus
came from God (16:30). They came to know God personally by accepting the word of
Christ. I also came to know God by studying the word of Christ. We all come to know
God through studying the word of God. Bible study and prayer are indeed two most
important things for us.
In verses 9-19, we learn Jesus’ prayer topics for his disciples:
First, “they may be one as we are one” (9-12). From the gospels, we get the
impression that Jesus’ disciples were not lovable people. They were slow to learn from
Jesus, but quick to compete with one another for human recognition. They ate and slept
a lot and did not pray much. They were mostly burdensome to Jesus. But Jesus did not
consider them useless. Jesus says, “And glory has come to me through them” (10). This
reveals Jesus’ heart. Jesus trained them in many ways to be future spiritual leaders. Still
they were too young. Soon they would abandon him upon his arrest. But Jesus prayed
for them. He saw them in God’s eyes. He had a hope that they would change the world
through their faith in God.
Look at verse 11b. “Holy Father, protect them by the power of your name, the
name you gave me, so that they may be one as we are one.” Jesus prayed that God
would protect them so that they would be united in him. Satan’s work is to destroy and
tear down relationships. God’s work is to build up and to unite. Jesus prayed that his
disciples might be united in him by obeying his words, as he was united with God by
obeying God’s command. We are, like the disciples, selfish and individualistic. So it is
not easy for us to be united. Only when we come to know the love of God we can be
united into one. Jesus is our common denominator. Jesus also prayed for the disciples
that they might conquer the world by being one in the love of God. We can be united in
Jesus when we pray for his world mission command with one accord. Yes, we are all
different in terms of our background, character, tastes, and so on. But just as many
different instruments in orchestra can make beautiful music, we can be united in Jesus’
name and serve him together humbly.

Second, “sanctify them by the truth” (13-19). Jesus was going back to God soon,
leaving his disciples in the world. The world would hate them, simply because they
followed the way of God instead of the sinful way of this world. Jesus did not want his
disciples to escape from the world. Rather he wanted them to challenge and conquer the
world with the word of God. He prayed that God might protect them from the evil one,
as he taught in the Lord’s Prayer, “Deliver us from evil.”
Look at verse 17. “Sanctify them by the truth; your word is truth.” Jesus prayed
that God would sanctify them by the truth. The word “sanctify” is from the Greek word
“hagiazo” () which means “to set apart for sacred use or to make holy.” Jesus
prayed that God would set them apart from the world and make them a holy people. We
learn that we should not be of the world, even though we are in the world. We should
not conform to this unbelieving world. As disciples of Jesus, we should have a different
value system, a different purpose in life.
God who is holy wants us to live a holy life. How can we be sanctified and live a
holy life in this unholy generation? Jesus prayed: “Sanctify them by the truth; your
word is truth.” We must hold on to God’s word, because God’s word is truth. Without
God’s word, we have no absolute standard. Without God’s word, we cannot but live
according to our sinful desires and social consensus which changes over time. Many
people just follow the trend of the world without questioning it. Today’s young people
live in a moral wilderness. Without the absolute word of God to guide them, they blindly
follow the trend of this world. Everything becomes relative. For example, statistics show
that a majority of young people now support gay marriage, even though the Bible
teaches us that homosexuality is a sin (Ro 1:26-27). Worse yet, many of them see no
problem with having sex outside marriage. But sin under any other name is still sin. Sin
brings condemnation and death to people, even when they commit it not recognizing as
sin. Again we learn how important it is for us to study the word of God faithfully so that
we may be sanctified by the truth.
We must be sanctified with the word of truth. This means that our inner persons
are transformed in Christ. Our hearts can be made pure and we can live a holy life only
when our sinful nature is changed in Christ. This can happen through the word of God.
To be sanctified, that is, to be set apart does not mean we should separate ourselves
from our society to live in a remote island or in a monastery. Rather, we should be in the
world among people to have a good spiritual influence on them. Jesus said, “My prayer
is not that you take them out of the world but that you protect them from the evil one.
They are not of the world, even as I am not of it” (15-16). How can we have good spiritual
influence on others? We can’t do this when we conform to the world. This is possible
only when we follow Christ by obeying the word of God.
Look at verse 19. “For them I sanctify myself, that they too may be truly
sanctified.” Jesus sanctified himself by obeying God’s will to die on the cross. In this way
Jesus taught us how we may be truly sanctified.

III. JESUS PRAYS FOR ALL BELIEVERS (20-26)
Jesus prayed also for all future believers who would come to him through his
disciples’ preaching. Let us think about two particular prayer topics of Jesus.
First, “all of them may be one.” Look at verse 21. “...that all of them may be one,
Father, just as you are in me and I am in you. May they also be in us so that the world
may believe that you have sent me.” This was the second time Jesus prayed for unity
among believers. This time he prayed for all future believers to be one. Again how can
we be united? We can do this when we have a right relationship with God through his
Son Jesus. When we have a personal relationship with God, we can also have a love
relationship with one another in him. When we love one another, the world may believe
in Jesus as the Son of God (22). Jesus prays in verse 23: “… so that they may be brought
to complete unity. Then the world will know that you sent me and have loved them
even as you have loved me.” Jesus teaches us that we can be most effective evangelists
to the world if we are united in him and be a spiritual orchestra that plays the beautiful
music of love.
Second, “to be with me where I am.” Look at verse 24. “Father, I want those you
have given me to be with me where I am, and to see my glory, the glory you have given
me because you loved me before the creation of the world.” Jesus prayed for all of us to
be with him in heaven after our pilgrimage on earth. Jesus loves each of us so much.
What does he want for us because he loves us? Jesus did not pray for the improvement
of our human conditions. He prayed for our names to be in the book of life and for us to
be the members of God’s family. Jesus said, “Father, I want those you have given me to
be with me where I am, and to see my glory.” Jesus wants us to enjoy the glory of the
kingdom of God with him forever. He concludes his prayer in verse 26: “I have made
you known to them, and will continue to make you known in order that the love you
have for me may be in them and that I myself may be in them.”
From the high priestly prayer of Jesus, we learn that Jesus wants us to glorify
God through our lives. He wants all of us to be with him forever in his glorious kingdom.
May God help us to live for the glory of God! May God sanctify us with his word of truth
and help us to be united in his love!
