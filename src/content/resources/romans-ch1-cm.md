A WORLD WITHOUT FAITH
Romans 1:18-32
Key Verse: 1:18
“The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the godlessness and
wickedness of people, who suppress the truth by their wickedness, …”
Last week, we learned that a sinner can be made right with God only by faith in
Jesus. The righteous will live by faith. Paul said, “I am not ashamed of the gospel,
because it is the power of God that brings salvation to everyone who believes: first to
the Jew, then to the Gentile” (16). Everyone needs the grace of salvation, because
everyone is a sinner. Both the Jew and the Gentile are in need of salvation.
Beginning from today’s passage, St. Paul describes the problems of two groups of
people in the world—the Jews and the Gentiles. The Jews represent those who were
blessed with God’s word as his chosen people. The Gentiles did not have the Bible. God’s
plan was to bless his chosen people to be a kingdom of priests who could share the word
of God with the Gentiles. This plan did not work out because the Jews rejected God’s
purpose for them. Anyway, Paul’s point is that both the Jews and the Gentiles need
Jesus for salvation. In today’s passage, Paul describes the spiritual reality of the Gentiles
who outright rejected God. Next week, he will address the problem of the Jews. As we
study today’s passage, let us pray that God may give us a broken shepherd’s heart for
those who reject the truth and are under God’s wrath.
I. GOD’S WRATH AGAINST SINFUL HUMANITY (18-20)
Look at verse 18. The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all the
godlessness and wickedness of people, who suppress the truth by their wickedness,…
Just as the righteousness from God is revealed in the gospel, so the wrath of God is
being revealed against those who are wicked. Our God is merciful and loving, but he is
also a righteous God. We must have a balanced view of God. He does not look at the
wickedness of people and say, “It’s no big deal. Don’t worry about it.” No. Our God is a
righteous God. Paul says that the wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all
the godlessness and wickedness of people. God is angry. He is very angry. But we must
understand that God’s wrath is different from our anger. God is not like an emotional
person who blows up at others because he is having a bad day. God’s anger is rooted in
his righteousness. The wrath of God is a fierce anger against evil. Notice that God's
wrath is being revealed not against people, but against the godlessness and wickedness of
people. It is true—God hates sin but loves the sinner. So let us erase the image of God from
our minds that he enjoys some sadistic joy of punishing people. We learn time and again
from our Bible studies that God’s punishment is redemptive, meaning, he punishes people
to help them to turn around from their wickedness. At the same time, we should remember
that God is a God of righteous judgment.
So why is God angry against the godlessness and wickedness of people? What does
it mean to be godless? To be wicked? To be godless is to live without God. To be wicked is

also to live without God. Paul says that the wicked people suppress the truth by their
wickedness. What does this mean? What truth do they suppress and why is it so wicked?
Verses 19, 20 and 25 give us a clue. They suppress the truth about the existence of God.
They deny the Creator God who is the source of their lives. They do not acknowledge God’s
presence in their daily life.
Now you may ask “What is so wicked about that?” Beheading an innocent person
and posting a video of it online, that is wicked. Punching a woman unconscious in the
elevator, that is wicked. Dumping toxic waste into a stream, that is wicked. But ignoring
God? Yes, the Bible teaches us that that is wicked. This is what we learn in Genesis 6: God
saw “how great the wickedness of the human race had become on the earth” that he
decided to wipe out the mankind with a great flood. What did they do? Did they murder
and rob each other daily? Well, this is how Jesus described their wickedness in Luke 17.
“Just as it was in the days of Noah, so also will it be in the days of the Son of Man. People
were eating, drinking, marrying and being given in marriage up to the day Noah entered
the ark. Then the flood came and destroyed them all” (Lk 17:26-27). God also destroyed
Sodom and Gomorrah for their wickedness. What did they do? This is what Jesus said. “It
was the same in the days of Lot. People were eating and drinking, buying and selling,
planting and building. But the day Lot left Sodom, fire and sulfur rained down from
heaven and destroyed them all (Lk 17:28-29).
Eating and drinking and marrying. Buying and selling. Planting and building. What
is so wicked about them? After all, we all do these things. The point is that they were
wicked because they lived their lives ignoring God the Creator. They suppressed the truth
about the existence of God. They were like some people today who claim that they would
be fine without God. They even say that humanity would be better off without God. The
wrath of God is being revealed against their wickedness.
Now some people who hear about the wrath of God may ask: “What? God is
angry with me because I ignore him? But I didn’t even know that he existed! How would
I know if he was there?” They are like some drivers who tell the state trooper, “Officer, I
didn’t know that the speed limit is 65 mph.” Pleading ignorance does not work. What is
Paul’s answer to them? Look at verses 19-20. “since what may be known about God is
plain to them, because God has made it plain to them. For since the creation of the
world God’s invisible qualities—his eternal power and divine nature—have been
clearly seen, being understood from what has been made, so that people are without
excuse.”
Paul says God has made himself known to mankind. People living in a remote
jungle would not know about Jesus and what he has done to save them (that is why we
must preach the gospel to them). But even they can know God. Since his creation, God
has revealed his invisible qualities for all of us to see so that people are without excuse.
The Bible says that anyone who seeks God with all his heart will find him (Jer 29:13).
Anyone with an open mind can see his eternal power and divine nature by just looking
at majestic mountains, lilies of the field, and the mesmerizing eyes of a baby. That is
why even some prominent scientists believe in God, even though they know all the
details of the theory of evolution. Astronaut John Glenn said, “To look out at this kind of

II. THEIR FOOLISH HEARTS WERE DARKENED (21-32)
Paul continues to teach us how the sinful humanity without God become
progressively worse, not better, as some intellectuals claim.
First, the root of sin (21). Look at verse 21. For although they knew God, they neither
glorified him as God nor gave thanks to him, but their thinking became futile and their
foolish hearts were darkened. God created the universe. He created humans as the
culmination of his creation. He created man and woman in his own image. And he was
very happy about that. He loved them and wanted them to be happy in him and with
him. Sadly, the humans he created did not glorify him as God nor gave thanks to him.
Instead, they rebelled against him. The root of all sin is pride and ingratitude. It
happened early in human history. Sin entered the world because of their pride and
ingratitude. Ever since then, we are under curse. People still say, “I don’t need God. I am
fine without him.” But we are not fine without God. Paul says that when people neither
glorified God as God nor gave thanks to him, their thinking became futile and their
foolish hearts were darkened. They did not become better. They became worse without
God. We should know this. My life cannot be better without God.
Second, foolish exchanges (22-27). Paul gives some examples of how people who
reject God become foolish, although they claim to be wise. A fool is one who does not
trade well, exchanging something good with something bad. Paul says that foolish
people without God make three foolish exchanges. (a) They exchanged the glory of the
immortal God for images made to look like a mortal human being and birds and
animals and retiles (23). (b) They exchanged the truth about God for a lie, and
worshiped and served created things rather than the Creator (25). This is true. Instead
of worshiping their Creator God who is eternal, people worship all kinds of objects as
their idols. Not only that, they worship money, power, fame, and even some Hollywood
movie stars as their idols. It is foolish. (c) They also exchanged natural sexual relations
for unnatural ones (26-27). Both men and women abandoned natural relations and were
inflamed with homosexual lusts. Paul calls their behavior “shameful acts.” I believe this
is offensive to God, just as any sexual sin is offensive to him, because it destroys the
family, a building block that God created for our happiness. To many of them, sex is
merely a way to enjoy physical pleasure. Now some young people may no longer
consider adultery and homosexuality as shameful acts in this day and age, calling them
alternative lifestyle. But we have to be careful. God made it clear that they are sins. His

words have not changed, even as our society has changed. To think that we can rewrite
the law of God according a social trend is to reject God as God.
Third, God gave them over (23-28). What did God do when he saw his beloved
humans rejected him to worship worthless idols and engage in sinful activities? The
phrase “God gave them over” is repeated three times here. God gave them over in the
sinful desires of their hearts to sexual impurity (24). God gave them over to shameful
lusts (26). God gave them over to a depraved mind (28). What does this mean? Did God
abandon the sinful humanity because they abandoned him? The truth is that God’s love for
us is deeper than that. God saw the sinful humanity’s deliberate rejection of him and his
truth. Notice the words that precede “God gave them over”—“Therefore” (24); “Because of
this” (26); and “Since they did not” (28). They persisted in their evil ways. So God gave
them over to their sinful ways of life. He allowed them to go their ways in the hope that
they would come back to their senses on their own. This reminds us of what the father did
in Jesus’ parable of the prodigal son in Luke 15. In the story, the younger son wanted to
take his share of inheritance to go to a foreign country to enjoy his life apart from his father.
The father knew what would happen to his beloved son. He knew that his son would suffer
in a faraway country without his love and protection. Still, the father gave him the money
and let him go his way. And from day 1, he waited eagerly at the gate for his son’s safe
return. This father is a picture of our God who allows sinners to go their own ways. In their
foolishness, people want to be far away from God, trying to enjoy the independent life from
him, ignoring him and rebelling against him. They don’t know how much God loves them.
They don’t know how God is waiting for their return.
Fourth, what a godless society is like (29-32). Finally in verses 29-31, Paul
compiles a list of sins that people commit when they live apart from God: They have
become filled with every kind of wickedness, evil, greed and depravity. They are full of
envy, murder, strife, deceit and malice. They are gossips, slanderers, God-haters,
insolent, arrogant and boastful; they invent ways of doing evil; they disobey their
parents; they have no understanding, no fidelity, no love, no mercy. I don’t think Paul
meant this to be a comprehensive list of all possible sins. He says people invent ways of
doing evil (30). That is true. People are still inventing ways to sin. We can think of many
modern-day sins to add to the list, such as insurance fraud, social media bullying,
identity theft … to name a few. The point is that the world does not become better
without God. It becomes progressively worse. Paul says in verse 32. Although they know
God’s righteous decree that those who do such things deserve death, they not only
continue to do these very things but also approve of those who practice them. When we
commit a sin first time, we usually feel guilty about it, because of the God-given
conscience. For example, when we lie to someone first time, we feel a tinge of guilt. But
we tend to suppress the guilty feeling and commit the sin again. After a while, we
become numb to the guilty feeling. We commit the same sin again and again. So we
continue to sin. Not only that, just as Eve wanted Adam to be her accomplice, we want
other people to join in our sinful acts, because it feels better if others do the bad things
as well. That is what the sinful people do. And the wrath of God is being revealed against
them.

In conclusion, a world without God is not a utopia as some people have us believe.
People claim to be wise, but when they live with God they do foolish things like
exchanging good for evil, doing things to hurt themselves and their families. The wrath
of God is being revealed against the wickedness of people who suppress the truth of God.
But the real reason why Paul describes the condition of the sinful humanity is this: He
wants us to know that we sinners desperately need the gospel that brings salvation to
everyone who believes in Jesus.
