DON’T BE AFRAID; JUST BELIEVE
Luke 8:40-56
Key Verse 8:50
Hearing this, Jesus said to Jairus, “Don’t be afraid; just believe, and she will be
healed.”
In today’s passage, Jesus helps two persons—a community leader named Jairus
and a nameless woman with a bleeding problem. Jesus helps both of them to grow in
their personal faith in him. We learn what kind of faith Jesus wants us to have. May God
help us to learn what it means to just believe!
I. JAIRUS, A GOOD FATHER (40-42)
After healing a demon-possessed man of the Gerasenes, Jesus returned to
Galilee. A crowd of people welcomed him, for they were all expecting him (40). They had
all kinds of problems in life and were expecting Jesus to help solve them. In particular, a
man had emergency at home and came to Jesus for help. Look at verses 41-42. Then a
man named Jairus, a ruler of the synagogue, came and fell at Jesus’ feet, pleading with
him to come to his house because his only daughter, a girl of about twelve, was dying.
As Jesus was on his way, the crowds almost crushed him. A large crowd of people
surrounded Jesus. He was like a rock star at a crowded concert or a pope visiting the city
of Philadelphia. But Jesus noticed one man in the crowd and responded to his earnest
plea.
Who was Jairus? He was a leader in his community, a ruler of the synagogue. He
was wealthy, with many servants. He had a comfortable home and a lovely wife. The
couple had only one daughter. So she was very precious to them. She was a girl of
twelve, pure and beautiful, like a flower about to blossom. Jairus was very happy
whenever she hugged him saying, “Daddy, I love you!” He called her “princess” and
showered her with many things. He had a long day at work, but as soon as he saw the
smiling face of his young daughter, all his tiredness disappeared. She was the apple of
his eye.
One day, a tragedy struck Jairus’ family. His daughter collapsed with a terrible
sickness. She was getting worse by the hour and was now on the verge of death. Jairus
wished that it were a nightmare and wanted to wake up. But it was reality. He called a
local doctor who came promptly but only shook his head in despair. As Jairus watched
his dying daughter, his heart sank. He could not bear the thought of living without his
daughter. He was overwhelmed by fear. Satan seized the opportunity and planted fear in
his heart.
In his sorrow, Jairus cried out to God. Then God helped him to remember Jesus.
He had heard about how Jesus healed the sick as he preached the kingdom of God.
Jesus had healed a man with leprosy, a paralytic, and Simon’s mother. This Jesus could
surely help his dying daughter. Jairus decided to come to Jesus and beg his mercy for

his daughter. However, it was not easy for him to come to Jesus. There were obstacles
that he had to overcome. First, he had to overcome the peer pressure from the religious
authorities. He was aware that the Pharisees had already branded Jesus as a law-
breaking cult leader. His coming to Jesus would mean going against the religious
leaders. We also face peer pressure when we try to come to Jesus. When you say that
you are going to Bible Café or worship service, your unbelieving friends may look at you
like you are strange. It takes courage for us to come to Jesus. Jairus overcame the peer

Jairus also had to overcome his pride. From a human point of view, Jesus was
only a young preacher at this time, while Jairus was an established leader of the
community. But Jairus curbed his pride. When he came to Jesus, he knelt down at
Jesus’ feet and pleaded with him to come to his home and heal his dying daughter.
Jairus was a great man because he was humble. The Bible says, “God opposes the proud
but gives grace to the humble” (Ja 4:6). Jesus accepted his humble prayer and went
with him. May God help us to be humble before the Lord!
Most of all, Jairus was a good father to his daughter. He overcame the peer
pressure and his own pride because he loved his daughter. Jairus loved her more than
himself. There was nothing he wouldn’t do for her. I am sure his love touched Jesus’
heart, because Jesus is always moved by a person’s caring and loving heart. Now one
might say, “But isn’t it natural for a father to love his own daughter?” It is true.
However, the reality is that we live in a society where many fathers abandon their sons
and daughters because of their sinful nature. Almost 4 out of 10 children in America do
not live with their fathers. Statistics show that 71% of all high school dropouts come
from fatherless homes. 90% of all homeless and runaway children are from fatherless
homes. 70% of juveniles in state institutions come from fatherless homes. Having
children does not automatically make men good fathers. They are only biological
fathers. Good fathers are those who have a father’s heart. Jairus was a good father. Most
all, our Father in heaven is the best father. Because of our sin, we were dying,
condemned in eternity. But our Father sent Jesus to die for our sins. This is the heart of
God, the heart of our eternal Father. Jesus was happy to see Jairus’ love and shepherd’s
heart for his daughter. He was on his way to Jairus’ house.
II. WHO TOUCHED ME? (43-48)
Look at verses 43-44. And a woman was there who had been subject to bleeding
for twelve years, but no one could heal her. She came up behind him and touched the
edge of his cloak, and immediately her bleeding stopped. In the large crowd of people,
there was a woman with a bleeding problem. She interrupted Jesus’ trip to Jairus’
house.
Who was this woman and how did she come to Jesus? According to Mark, she
went to many different doctors and spent all her money, but no one could help her (Mk
5:26). Her bleeding problem continued for 12 years. Many things happened during those
12 years. All her high school friends got married and had several kids. But her bleeding
never stopped. She had spent her youthful years, continually suffering from her chronic

disease. Her physical problem caused her to suffer many other problems as well. For one
thing, her condition made her a social outcast, because she was regarded as
ceremonially unclean (Lev 15:25). It meant that she could not go to any public places,
because her illness would taint others, she was told. She suffered not only physically, but
also emotionally, socially and financially. We can imagine how sorrowful and fatalistic
she could have become. She had no hope for future. And we know that hopelessness kills
a person from within. But one day she heard wonderful news that changed her life
forever. She heard that Jesus of Nazareth healed a man with leprosy, drove out demons
from a crazy man, and raised a widow’s dead son. When she heard this news, a ray of
hope glimmered in her heart. She realized that this Jesus could heal her sickness and
give her a new life. She decided to come to Jesus.
However, as in the case of Jairus, she had to overcome many obstacles in order to
come to Jesus. The Jewish law forbade her to have even casual contacts with others so
as not to make them ceremonially unclean (Lev 15:27). Besides, they would be offended
by the unpleasant odor emanating from her bleeding problem. But when she made a
decision of faith to come to Jesus, God gave her the courage to overcome the obstacles.
Her coming to Jesus was an act of faith.
So she came to Jesus by faith. Still she could not come publicly. So she came up
with a secret plan. No one should know what she was about to do, not even Jesus. She
believed that, based on what she heard about the power of Jesus, she would be healed
with a fraction of his power. She said to herself, “I will come up behind him and touch
the edge of his tunic. That will be sufficient. I will be healed of this debilitating disease. I
can be normal again. I can even marry and live happily ever after.” She was excited
about her plan. In her mind, there was only one problem in her life—the constant
bleeding. Her skin was dry all the time. Her complexion was bad because of the
problem. She couldn’t get a job because of that. She couldn’t get married because of that.
And so on. All her life problems would be gone if only she were healed of the terrible
disease!
So she carried out her plan. She came up behind Jesus and touched the edge of
his cloak. Then something wonderful happened. The bleeding inside her stopped
immediately. She was made clean by the power of the Son of God. She received a new
life. Jesus’ divine power went out to her, and the suffering woman was healed
completely! She was so happy that she wanted to run to her family and share the good
news. But there was a problem with this “hit and run” strategy.
Look at verse 45. “Who touched me?” Jesus asked. When they all denied it, Peter
said, “Master, the people are crowding and pressing against you.” Peter was puzzled by
Jesus’ question, “Who touched me?” because a large crowd of people was milling around
him. But Jesus answered, “Someone touched me; I know that power has gone out from
me” (46). Jesus would not allow the woman to slip away from the crowd unnoticed. Of
course, Jesus did not intend to embarrass her. Instead, Jesus wanted to help her further.
She was healed physically, but she needed a spiritual healing. In Jesus’ eyes, the
bleeding problem was not the most serious problem in her life. She needed to have a
personal relationship with the Lord. If she had gone home without establishing a

personal relationship with Jesus, soon she would forget God’s grace and become a
woman of bitterness again.
There was a man who had suffered from paralysis for 38 years. By his mercy,
Jesus healed the man. But after his healing, he forgot what Jesus had done for him. In
order to please the religious authorities, the man reported Jesus to the Jewish police,
telling them that he was the one who had violated the Sabbath law (Jn 5:15). The man
was healed physically, but he was still sick with a spiritual paralysis. When people suffer
from a physical sickness, they mistakenly think that everything will be okay only if they
are cured from the disease. However, physical healing is temporary as people die sooner
or later. Therefore, it was essential for this woman to receive a spiritual healing.
Jesus knew that it was not easy for the woman to come forward publicly. It was
painful for her to talk about her shameful bleeding problem before many people. But it
was necessary for her to make a confession of faith so that a spiritual healing process
might begin in her deep wounds. Romans 10:9 says, That if you confess with your
mouth, “Jesus is Lord,” and believe in your heart that God raised him from dead, you
will be saved.
What did the woman do? Look at verse 47. Then the woman, seeing that she
could not go unnoticed, came trembling and fell at his feet. In the presence of all the
people, she told why she had touched him and how she had been instantly healed. In
her personal testimony sharing, the woman probably said, “You know, I was a woman
with a bleeding problem. I had no hope in my life. I had no hope of getting married. But
by God’s grace, I am a new person!” Here we learn that it is important to share the grace
of God publicly. Some people do not like the idea of sharing a personal testimony, saying
that it should be private. But if we truly appreciate the grace of God in our life, we can’t
just keep it to ourselves. We want to climb on a mountaintop and shout to the world
what Jesus has done. There is no shame in sharing our terrible past because we have
new hope and vision for future in Jesus Christ.
Jesus was happy to see her beautiful act of faith. Look at verse 48. Then he said to
her, “Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace.” Jesus admired her faith and
blessed her with God’s peace. She was not only healed from a bleeding problem, but
received the peace of God. This is what happen to us if we come to Jesus by faith!
III. DON’T BE AFRAID; JUST BELIEVE (49-56)
While Jesus was talking to the woman, Jairus was waiting patiently for Jesus to
go to his house and take care of his daughter. Time was very critical to his dying
daughter. But the woman delayed the journey. Jairus’ patience was growing thin.
Finally, it happened. He got the news that he was afraid of hearing. Look at verse 49.
While Jesus was still speaking, someone came from the house of Jairus, the synagogue
ruler. “Your daughter is dead,” he said. “Don’t bother the teacher any more.” We can
imagine how Jairus must have felt. This was the news that took away any kind of hope
Jairus had. And the advice not to bother Jesus any more seemed a final nail on the
coffin. It sounded reasonable, too. The messenger was saying: “You did your best for

your daughter. Perhaps Jesus could have done something while she was alive. Now she
is dead and nothing can be done for her.” It was all over, and he should say goodbye to
Jesus and go home to prepare for her funeral. Humanly speaking this was a reasonable
assessment of his situation. But Jesus was still with Jairus. How did Jesus help him?
Let us read verse 50 together. Hearing this, Jesus said to Jairus, “Don’t be
afraid; just believe, and she will be healed.” Jesus understood what Jairus was going
through. Jairus had real fear—the fear of living without his daughter. But Jesus told
him, “Don’t be afraid; just believe, and she will be healed.” Now Jairus had two
conflicting words--the messenger’s advice and Jesus’ words of encouragement. He had a
choice to make. On the one hand, the messenger’s assessment seemed objective and
logical. But it was not based on faith in Jesus. It planted fear in his heart. On the other
hand, Jesus told him not to be afraid. Jesus did not even acknowledge his daughter’s
death. He said, “…she will be healed.” Could Jesus still help her even at this stage? A lot
of things were going through Jairus’ mind.
In the course of our lives, we often face the same kind of choice that Jairus had to
make. When we look at our human situation, we are tempted to throw our hands up and
quit. Satan takes the opportunity and plants fear in our hearts. He says, “Jesus cannot
do anything for you; just give up.” But we must hear the words of Jesus, who says,
“Don’t be afraid; just believe.” Jesus wants us to have faith in him even when things look
impossible.
What did Jairus decide? Did he follow the messenger’s advice or did he decide to
put his trust in Jesus? The author Luke does not mention what he said. Perhaps he
didn’t say anything. But his action spoke loudly. He followed Jesus. He made a decision
to listen to Jesus instead of the messenger. He believed that Jesus could help his
daughter. Faith is to believe in God when it is difficult to believe. Jairus had faith in
Jesus. We learn three things about faith.
First, waiting on God by faith. Jesus’ trip to his house was interrupted by the
woman. Jairus waited very patiently for Jesus. His conversation with the woman
seemed to be interminable to Jairus. He felt that his blood pressure was going up and he
was about to faint. Still, Jairus was waiting on Jesus quietly and patiently. It was an
expression of his faith.
Second, decision of faith. Some people say they believe in God, but when it comes to
decision time, they choose the world, not God. That is not genuine faith. Jesus said, “If
you love me, you will obey what I command” (Jn 14:15). What we do, not just what we
say, reveals our faith. Jairus had two choices—following the reasonable advice of the
messenger or the promise of Jesus. He made a decision of faith. May God help us to
make decisions of faith!
Third, “just believing” faith. At the news that his daughter was dead, Jairus could
have given up and said, “It is all over.” Many people begin to panic in such a situation.
But a person with faith holds on to God’s promise in times of difficulty. Jairus was a
man of faith. When Jesus said to him, “Don’t be afraid; just believe,” he just believed

Jesus’ words. What does it mean to just believe? A shoe company slogan says we should
“just do it.” Anti-drug campaigners say “Just say no.” But Jesus says “Just believe.” It
means to believe in Jesus’ promise despite our own feelings, despite our own logic, and
despite what the advice of the reasonable people around us. In order to believe Jesus’
words, Jairus had to fight hard against doubts that Satan was planting in his heart. His
heart was becoming numb when he thought about his daughter. But he decided to
believe in Jesus’ word by faith. He listened to Jesus’ word, “Don’t be afraid; just believe.”
He could win the spiritual victory when he just believed in Jesus’ words.
Look at verses 51-53. When he arrived at the house of Jairus, he did not let
anyone go in with him except Peter, John and James, and the child’s father and
mother. Meanwhile, all the people were wailing and mourning for her. “Stop wailing,”
Jesus said. “She is not dead but asleep.” They laughed at him, knowing that she was
dead. When Jesus arrived at the house of Jairus, he only allowed those who were
prepared to witness his power over death. He took Peter, John and James, and the
child’s parents and went into the house. The townspeople were gathered there,
mourning for the dead girl. They were overwhelmed by the tragedy that happened to the
nice family and by their helplessness before the power of death. But Jesus told them,
“Stop wailing. She is not dead but asleep.” Jesus said this because he is the source of life
and there is no death in Jesus (Jn 11:25).
At his word, people began to laugh at him—”Ha, ha, ha”—for they knew she was
dead in fact. They had the girl’s cold body to prove it. The unbelieving atmosphere
hardened people’s hearts and they became cynical and rebellious toward Jesus. But
Jesus took the little girl by the hand and said, “My child, get up!” Her spirit returned,
and at once she stood up (55a). In this way, Jesus revealed the glory of God. When
Jairus overcame his fear by faith and just believed, he could experience the power of
faith in God Almighty. Jesus knew that teenagers are always hungry, so he told them to
give her something to eat.
Today we learned that we have to make a decision of faith to obey Jesus’ word in
order to live a victorious life. May God grant us faith that overcomes fear! May God help
us just believe in Jesus in times of distress and experience the life-giving power of our
Lord Jesus!
