OPEN YOUR EYES AND LOOK AT THE FIELDS!
John 4:27-42
Key Verse: 4:35
“Do you not say, ‘Four months more and then the harvest’? I tell you, open your
eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest.”
Last week, we learned how Jesus helped a Samaritan woman to come to know the
Messiah through his patient Bible study with her. Jesus led her to a spring of water welling
up to eternal life (15). Finally, Jesus declared to her that he was the Messiah she had been
waiting for (26). Today’s passage tells us about the fruit of Jesus’ labor of love for the
woman. Soon after meeting Jesus personally, the woman became an evangelist for her
people. In this passage, Jesus also teaches us how we can have a world mission vision.
I. “COME, SEE A MAN WHO TOLD ME EVERYTHING” (27-30)
Look at verse 27. Just then his disciples returned and were surprised to find him
talking with a woman. But no one asked, “What do you want?” or “Why are you talking
with her?” Let me recap the first part of this chapter. We learned how Jesus broke down
the human barriers between Jews and Samaritans in order to reach out to one sinful
woman. First, he went through Samaria even though Jews avoided the region at any cost
(4). Then he initiated a conversation with a lonely woman by humbly asking her the
question, “Will you give me a drink?” This woman was shunned by her own people
because of her immoral life. But Jesus talked to her gently. This could have caused a
scandal in the Jewish community, because Jews did not associate with Samaritans, let
alone female Samaritan.
The disciples of Jesus had followed him to Samaria. But they had an uneasy feeling
as they passed through the Samaritan villages. This is evident when we read other gospels.
One time, Jesus and his disciples were passing through Samaria. Noticing that the
Samaritans did not welcome them, John asked Jesus, “Lord, do you want us to call fire
down from heaven and destroy them?” (Lk 9:54) No doubt Jesus’ disciples had a strong
anti-Samaritan sentiment.
After a long journey, they came to Sychar, Samaria, and it was noon. They had no
food with them. Their stomachs began to growl. They hated the idea of going into the
village to buy food from Samaritans. But they had no choice. Reluctantly they went into
the town and bought some food without an incidence. And they came back to Jesus with
joy. They were happy thinking about sharing the food with him. But when they returned,
they found something shocking. Jesus was talking with a Samaritan woman. They were
flabbergasted by the picture. But no one asked, “What do you want?” or “Why are you
talking with her?” (27) They were too surprised to ask him a question.
Look at verses 28-30. Then, leaving her water jar, the woman went back to the
town and said to the people, “Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could

She was truly free. The living water of Jesus brought a fundamental change in her life. Let
us think about this change.
First, she left her water jar behind. The water jar was a symbol of her thirsty life. It
was also a means of quenching her physical thirst. But she left the water jar behind and
ran to her people. This shows that she was no longer
thirsty in her soul. She drank the living water welling
up to eternal life and she was finally satisfied. She now
had a purpose and a meaning in her life. This reminds
me of a couple of young people who made similar
decisions. One young man used to go to bar to drink
and pick up woman. It was his Jacob’s well. But when
he tasted the living water of Jesus through his Bible
study, he said goodbye to his sinful past. A friend of
my younger daughter had enjoyed country music very much. It was her Jacob’s well. After
meeting Jesus personally, however, she discarded all of her CDs and decided to devote her
life to Jesus.
Second, the living water from Jesus changed her from a sinful woman to a
blessed woman of God. In the past, her life was a series of painful moments. She was
hurt by her ex-husbands who left big holes in her heart. She was judged and shunned by
her own people. She could not associate with the fellow Samaritan women in her village.
But everything changed from the moment Jesus came into her life. The Messiah was there
for her. He loved her despite her sinful past. He forgave her sins. She was full of joy in her
heart. She was full of hope. Her relationship with God changed. And her relationship with
people changed. She was now full of spirit to go back to her townspeople. She was no
longer lonely. She wanted to share the joy of knowing Jesus with her people.
Third, she was compelled to evangelize her townspeople. Look at verse 29.
“Come, see a man who told me everything I ever did. Could this be the Christ?” Her soul
rejoiced in God her Savior. So she ran to the townspeople. She told them, “I’ve met this
person who truly understands me. He knows everything about me and yet does not
condemn me. Instead, he saved me from all my sins through his living water. Who else
could be the Messiah but this Jesus!” She shared her life testimony before them with tears
of joy in her eyes. She was free to talk about her life now. John 8:32 says, “Then you will
know the truth, and the truth will set you free.” The living water welling up to eternal life

II. “OPEN YOUR EYES AND LOOK AT THE FIELDS!” (31-38)
Look at verses 31-33. Meanwhile his disciples urged him, “Rabbi, eat something.”
But he said to them, “I have food to eat that you know nothing about.” Then his disciples
said to each other, “Could someone have brought him food?” When the disciples saw
Jesus wasn’t interested in the food they brought, they wondered if someone had already
served him some food. What did Jesus tell them? Look at verse 34. “My food,” said Jesus,
“is to do the will of him who sent me and to finish his work.” When Jesus saw the
Samaritan woman starting a new life in God, he was so happy that he didn’t feel hungry
anymore. His heart was full of joy when he saw the change in the thirsty woman. She was
so pretty and happy, and Jesus was happy. To Jesus, to do the will of God by helping a
thirsty woman with the word of God was more satisfying than eating food. Praise Jesus the
true shepherd!
The beautiful change in the Samaritan woman reminded Jesus many others men
and women who were still suffering under the bondage of their sins. Look at verse 35.
Jesus and his disciples were looking at grain fields nearby. The harvest season was still
four months away. But Jesus said to them, “Do you not say, ‘Four months more and then
the harvest’? I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest.”
This verse shows the heart of our Lord Jesus Christ. Jesus had compassion for sinful men
and women of the world. Jesus saw how the Samaritan woman drank the living water
welling up to eternal life and began a new life. Then his heart went out for all the
Samaritan men and women of the world. Jesus wanted to harvest all sinners of the world
and bring them to God.
Who are the Samaritan men and women in our society? Many young people are
thirsty for human love and pleasures. They come to draw water in the wee hours of the
morning. They go to parties hoping to quench their thirst and to fill their emptiness. But
no alcoholic beverages, no boyfriend or girlfriend, no human being for that matter, can
truly satisfy them. Many times they suffer from bitter aftertaste. Only the living water

from Jesus can satisfy them. Only Jesus the Savior can give them eternal life. Jesus wants
us to see each and everyone of these thirsty souls with the eyes of compassion, because
they are like crops ripe for harvest.
Look at verse 35 again. Do you not say, ‘Four months more and then the harvest’?
I tell you, open your eyes and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest. Jesus had a
world mission vision. He was very happy when one woman accepted the Messiah and ran
to her townspeople to talk about what Jesus had done for her. This sinful woman accepted
Jesus as her personal Savior through her Bible study with Jesus and became a missionary
to her townspeople. What did Jesus see through this event? He saw a vision of reaching
out many sinful men and women of the world through personal Bible study like the one he
had with the Samaritan woman.
Jesus also wants us to have a world mission
vision. He gave us the world mission command,
“Go into all the world and preach the good news to
all creation” (Mk 16:15). There are seven billion
people in the world. How can we ever reach out to
all these people? We learn from Jesus that we can
begin our world mission by studying the word of
God with one person. World mission begins with
one person.
Look at verses 36-37. “Even now the reaper draws his wages, even now he
harvests the crop for eternal life, so that the sower and the reaper may be glad together.
Thus the saying ‘One sows and another reaps’ is true.” Jesus urges us to participate in the
harvesting of the lost souls. Jesus says that we are harvesters. We are not farmers, so what
do we harvest? We harvest “the crop for eternal life.” The Samaritan woman had been lost.
She was under the bondage of sin and eternal death. But Jesus led her to the living water
welling up to eternal life through his patient Bible study with her. Likewise, we can
participate in the soul harvesting work by sharing the gospel of salvation with young
people of our time. Jesus also says that there are sowers and there are reapers. Sometimes,
one sows and another reaps the crop. But the sower and reaper are glad together when a
soul is harvested for eternal life. God’s redemptive work is not done by one person. Jesus
encourages us that we should work together as the harvesters of lost souls.
In verses 37-38, Jesus alludes that John the Baptist was his co-harvester. John
sowed the seed of the gospel before the coming of Christ through his message of the
baptism of repentance. Now Jesus wanted his disciples to work hard as John the Baptist
did. Jesus also taught them a sense of history. God spread the seeds for the harvest,
beginning with Abraham, and through many prophets, all the way until John the Baptist.
Still there are many Samaritan men and women around us today. It is easy for us to see
them with the eyes of scorn and disdain for their selfish and immoral life. But we must see
their inner agony as Jesus saw the Samaritan woman. We must help them to open their
spiritual eyes and meet the Messiah personally through our Bible study with them.
III. SAMARITANS’ PERSONAL FAITH IN JESUS (39-42)

Look at verse 39. “Many of the Samaritans from that town believed in him
because of the woman’s testimony.” The Samaritan woman used to be the butt of jokes
among the people in her town. But something amazing happened in her. She was changed
180 degree. She was a new woman. She had an angelic smile on her face. Her testimony
was very moving. She told them how her life was going nowhere when she only craved for
human love from men. She talked about how she felt lonely and thirsty. Then she met
Jesus. Jesus knew all about her past life, but did not condemn her. She told them she had
accepted Jesus as Christ, her personal Savior and she was happy. Many Samaritans
believed in Jesus after hearing her testimony. Then they decided to invite Jesus to come
and stay with them. They held a two-day Bible conference with Jesus as the main
messenger. Jesus stayed with them, teaching them the word of God. Because of Jesus’
words, many more became believers. They wanted to drink the living water welling up to
eternal life as the Samaritan woman had done. The woman used to be a source of gossip
when she followed her sinful desires. Now she was a spiritual role model for many people.
The changed life of the woman was a powerful testimony to the Samaritans. May God help
us to be powerful witnesses to many young people of our time through our testimonies
and changed lives!
The Samaritan woman was instrumental in bringing the townspeople to Christ. But
faith is more than emotional reaction to someone’s moving testimony. Faith is a personal
relationship with the Lord through personal experience. Look at verse 42. They said to the
woman, “We no longer believe just because of what you said; now we have heard for
ourselves, and we know that this man really is the Savior of the world.” They first came
to Jesus because of the woman’s testimony. But that was just a beginning. After spending
time with Jesus, studying the Bible with him, they now had personal faith in him. This is
very important. Faith is very personal. We all want go to heaven. But each person is saved
by his or her own personal faith. A son cannot be saved because of his father’s faith. A wife
cannot go to heaven because of her husband’s faith (Jn 1:13).
The Samaritans became the children of God by personally believing in the word of
Jesus. Personal faith in the word of God can have a far-reaching influence on others. John
Knox (c.1505-1572) was ordained as priest in the Catholic Church in Scotland at the time
when John Calvin began the Reformation in Geneva. But after witnessing the corruption
of church leaders, he was confused about what to believe. So he studied the Bible diligently
and became a spiritual leader of the nation. However, when Mary Queen of Scots began
to re-establish Roman Catholicism, Knox was forced to leave the country. He went to
Geneva, where he stayed with John Calvin. He became an ancestor of the Puritans. He
boldly rebuked Mary for supporting catholic practices. His personal faith became so
influential that the Queen was afraid of him. Mary Queen of Scots is reputed to have said:
“I fear the prayers of John Knox more than all the assembled armies of Europe.” If one
has a personal faith, God can use him to bring about a great spiritual awakening.
In today’s passage, we learn Jesus’ vision for the world. “I tell you, open your eyes
and look at the fields! They are ripe for harvest.” Through one sinful woman’s conversion,
Jesus saw a vision to evangelize the whole world. May God grant us this vision of God and
help us begin our world mission by studying the word of God with one friend!
