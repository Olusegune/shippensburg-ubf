JESUS, THE BREAD OF LIFE
John 6:22-40
Key Verse: 6:35
Then Jesus declared, “I am the bread of life. He who comes to me will never go
hungry, and he who believes in me will never be thirsty.”
In today’s passage, Jesus declares that he is the bread of life. Ever since the fall of
man in the Garden of Eden, the bread problem has been the focal point of human life.
George Orwell (1903-50), the author of Animal Farm and 1984, had an encounter that
offered a graphic image of this problem. While he was eating, a wasp flew in and landed
on his plate and was sucking his jam. He cut the insect in half with knife. But it paid no
attention and kept on sucking the jam, while a tiny stream of jam trickled out of its
severed stomach. Only when it tried to fly away did it realize what had happened. The
wasp and people without Christ have much in common. Everyone has the bread problem
because everyone has to eat to survive. How can we overcome this problem of life? Jesus
declares that he is the bread of life. Jesus is the answer to our bread problem. May God
help us to know what it means that Jesus is the bread of life!
I. “DO NOT WORK FOR FOOD THAT SPOILS” (22-34)
Jesus fed the crowd of 5,000 with the five loaves and two fish, and they were fully
satisfied. They were happy to eat the free meal. Through this event, they should have
realized that Jesus is the Son of God. They should have faith in Jesus. Sadly, they failed
to see the spiritual meaning of his miracle. Rather, they saw Jesus as a free baker. They
wanted to make him a king so that they did not have to struggle with the bread problem
(15). It seemed to them that Jesus would make a perfect social welfare program. So
when Jesus quietly slipped away from the east side of the lake, they became restless not
knowing where Jesus was. They searched for him all night. They finally found him in
Capernaum, on the west side of the lake. They asked him, “Rabbi, when did you get
here?” (25) They were glad to see Jesus, hoping that they had a second chance to make
him a king who could supply them bread permanently.
What did Jesus say to these people? Look at verse 26. “I tell you the truth, you
are looking for me, not because you saw miraculous signs but because you ate the
loaves and had your fill.” Jesus was sorry because they had completely missed the point
of the miracle. Their eyes were on bread, not on Jesus. They were like a child who is so
excited about a toy gift that he forgets who gave the gift. The crowd had come to Jesus
the first time with a spiritual motive. They came because they had seen the miraculous
signs that Jesus performed. They came to see the Messiah. After getting the free meal
from Jesus, however, they began to focus on bread only, not the Messiah. When they
came to Jesus the second time, they came with bread problem on their minds, and no
more.
Jesus knew that they were working so hard for bread. They were desperate. What
did Jesus say to them? Look at verse 27. “Do not work for food that spoils, but for food

that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you. On him God the Father
has placed his seal of approval.” This verse reminds us of Matthew 4:4: Jesus
answered, “It is written: ‘Man does not live on bread alone, but on every word that

What then is bread? Bread represents the food that is necessary to sustain our
physical life. This problem has been a life problem of mankind throughout human
history. Everybody needs bread to survive, so people struggle very hard to get a little
more bread. And there seems to be no end in this struggle. Think about a typical
American man. He begins to work for bread in high school, if not earlier. He works all
his life to pay for his food, house, car, appliances, education for his children, and so on.
He works hard to save as much as he can so that he can retire in Florida or Arizona.
Almost all his life is devoted to working for bread.
In Jesus’ times, the Israelites suffered even more because of the Roman Empire’s
economic and political exploitation. What little they earned was taxed away by Rome for
its military expenses and luxuries for its citizens. They lived in a recession that seemed
to last without an end in sight. In this sense we can understand why they were so
preoccupied with their bread problem and why they were so excited to find someone
who could give them free bread everyday. But the sad fact is that even if people solve
their physical bread problem, they cannot save their lives with bread. Man is a spiritual
being as well as a physical being. He cannot live on bread alone (Dt 8:3). He needs to
eat, but he cannot be truly happy just because his stomach is full. The more serious
problem facing mankind is sin that kills the soul of a person. People who are under the
power of sin and death cannot be happy, even if they have all the bread they need.
Physical bread or money, no matter how much we have, cannot bring us out of the
darkness of sin and death. It cannot save us from eternal condemnation.
That is why Jesus said, “Do not work for food that spoils, but for food that
endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you.” This verse teaches us an
important truth. A life spent only toiling for food that spoils is not a blessed life.
According to Genesis 3:17, it is a cursed life. Many people spend their entire life working
only for food that spoils, without knowing that such life is a cursed life. When God
created man, he created him to do God’s mission. Man did not have to work for his own
food (Ge 1:30). But after the Fall, man was cursed to work hard for his food (Ge 1:28;
3:17).
So working for only three meals a day is a cursed life. Then how can we overcome
this cursed life? After all, all of us have to work for food in order to survive physically.
How can this problem be solved from the root? The Israelites in this passage thought
that their problem would be gone once they had a free bakery. Many people today also
view this only as a political or social problem. Democrats argue that generous social
programs are the answer. Republicans argue that “the trickle-down” economic policy is
the answer. But can the politicians really provide the answer to our bread problem?
Even if their economic plans secure our financial future, which is unlikely, we can be
sure they would not solve our life problem. It is because human desire for material
things is unlimited. More importantly the sin problem would remain unsolved.

Let’s read verse 27. “Do not work for food that spoils, but for food that endures
to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you. On him God the Father has placed
his seal of approval.” Jesus is the answer. Man cannot solve his life problem just by
working hard to preserve his mortal body. But those who have Jesus in their life can
solve their life problem. Jesus gives us eternal life; those who have eternal life have no
“life problem.” Those who have Jesus’ life in them can have real peace from heaven.
They can experience the kingdom of God, where there is no frustration or bloody
fighting for survival. To have Jesus’ life in us is the only solution to our life problems.
Just as we need bread to sustain our physical body, we need Jesus to sustain our
spiritual life.
At first glance, Jesus’ words in verse 27 are puzzling. He says we should not work
for food that spoils. Does this mean we should all quit our jobs? He then says we should
work for food that endures to eternal life. What a minute. Isn’t eternal life a free gift that
we cannot earn? Actually the first part is easy. We should not quit our jobs. In fact, we
should work hard. The Bible says that “The one who is unwilling to work shall not eat”
(2Th 3:10). We should work hard to support our family but this kind of work should not
be the main goal of our life. Now on the second point about working for food that
endures to eternal life. Jesus teaches us that we should work not only for physical bread,
but also for food that endures to eternal life. What does it mean to work for food that
endures to eternal life? Look at verses 28-29. When they asked Jesus, “What must we do
to do the works God requires?” he answered, “The work of God is this: to believe in the
one he has sent.” Faith in Jesus is the answer to our life problem because he gives us
eternal life. There is no one who can save us from our sins but Jesus. To believe in Jesus
is to believe personally that Jesus died for our sins on the cross, and that we will rise
again to be with Jesus on the last day. To work for food that endures to eternal life is to
know Jesus personally and to obey his words. It is to live a life of faith and mission in
Jesus.
“Do not work for food that spoils, but for food that endures to eternal life, which
the Son of Man will give you.” Let me repeat: Jesus is the answer to our life problem
because only he can give us eternal life. May God help us to work for food that endures
to eternal life!
II. “I AM THE BREAD OF LIFE” (35-40)
Look at verse 35. Then Jesus declared, “I am the bread of life. He who comes to
me will never go hungry, and he who believes in me will never be thirsty.” The crowd
thought that their problem was the lack of bread on their dinner table. They wanted
Jesus to solve their physical bread problem. But Jesus drew their attention to a more
urgent problem—the salvation of their souls.
First, Jesus satisfies our hunger (35). What does it mean Jesus is the bread of life?
It means that Jesus came down from heaven to solve our bread problem. In this passage,
the words “from heaven” are repeated 10 times (31,32a,32b,33,38,41,42,50,51,58). This
repetition has a spiritual significance. No one or nothing from this world can solve the

fundamental problem of the sinful mankind. The solution must come from God.
Matthew 1:21 explains this truth. It says, “She will give birth to a son, and you are to
give him the name Jesus, because he will save his people from their sins.” Jesus came
from heaven to be our Savior. C.S. Lewis (1898-1963) said that Jesus’ claim to come
from heaven makes him one of 3 things: he is a lunatic, a liar, or the Lord of the
universe. He cannot be reduced only to a good moral teacher or a prophet. We must be
very clear about who Jesus is to us as we follow him.
In the midst of material abundance, there are many hungry and thirsty souls.
Many are hungry for parent’s love. They are hungry for fame and wealth. They are
hungry for power. They are thirsty for worldly achievements. They are thirsty and weary
from their hard work. Sometimes we see on TV the malnourished children in Africa and
North Korea suffering from severe famine. They say that hunger makes people most
sorrowful. But there is another kind of hunger. Hunger and thirst in this passage
represent man’s tiredness and weariness in their souls. Why are they so tired and weary?
The Bible says they are tired and weary because they carry the heavy burden of sin, and
because they are under the torment of Satan. This kind of hunger and thirst cannot be
relieved by eating food or by drinking beverages. Only Jesus can satisfy their souls
because he came from heaven to save people from their sins. Jesus says in verse 33. “For
the bread of God is he who comes down from heaven and gives life to the world.”
In Luke 12, Jesus tells the parable of the rich fool to teach how fleeting it is to
struggle for only physical bread (Lk 12:13-21). A man worked very hard to be materially
successful. After many years of hard labor, he finally became rich. One year he struck a
gold mine when his ground produced an unusually good crop. He thought to himself,
“What shall I do? I have no place to store my crops.” He could not sleep at night partly
because of excitement and partly because he was worried about his wealth. Then he said,
“This is what I’ll do. I will tear down my barns and build bigger ones, and there I will
store all my grain and my goods. And I’ll say to myself, ‘You have plenty of good things
laid up for many years. Take life easy; eat, drink and be merry.’” This man had no
concept of a spiritual life. When he was blessed with wealth, all he could think about was
how to live a pleasure seeking life. But God had news for him. He said to the man, “You
fool! This very night your life will be demanded from you. Then who will get what you
have prepared for yourself?” What good is an impressive fleet of BMW’s, heated
swimming pools, vacations homes, or a portfolio of stocks and bonds when a man’s life
is finished? Jesus asks in Mark 8:36, “What good is it for a man to gain the whole
world, yet forfeit his soul?” We need Jesus who came down from heaven to give us
eternal life.
Second, the will of God to save the world (36-40). Jesus is the bread of life. He
who comes to Jesus will never go hungry, and he who believes in Jesus will never be
thirsty. But the crowd did not believe because of their fixed idea that physical bread was
the answer to their life problems. Then Jesus told them about God’s will so that
somehow they might come to him. Look at verse 39. “And this is the will of him who
sent me, that I shall lose none of all that he has given me, but raise them up at the last
day.” God’s will is that he wants to save all people through his Son Jesus Christ. God is
like a shepherd who leaves 99 sheep in the open country to find one lost sheep. God’s
