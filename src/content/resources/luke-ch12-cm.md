BE READY FOR THE SECOND COMING OF JESUS
Luke 12:35-59
Key Verse: 12:40
“You also must be ready, because the Son of Man will come at an hour when you
do not expect him.”
In today’s passage, Jesus tells us that we must be ready for his Second Coming.
This world as we know it will not continue, as many people assume. Jesus will come
again one day to judge the living and the dead, and to establish a new heaven and a new
earth. This will certainly happen, but we don’t know exactly when. So we must be ready.
Unfortunately, not everyone will be ready for it. It will be a terrible day for those who are
not ready. May God help each of us to be ready!
I. BE READY (35-48)
Look at verses 35-36. “Be dressed ready for service and keep your lamps
burning, like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding banquet, so
that when he comes and knocks they can immediately open the door for him.” Jesus
teaches us what kind of life we should live as we wait for his Second Coming. We must
be like servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding banquet. In Jesus’
time, a wedding banquet lasted for a long time, maybe until the wine ran out. So the
servants did not know exactly when their master would return. He could come early, or
much later. It was impossible for them to predict the time. So they had to be ready. As
soon as the master knocked, they must immediately open the door for him. They could
not do this, if they fell asleep or held a wild party. Likewise, we will miss Jesus’ coming if
we are not ready. Jesus said two things: first, to be dressed ready for service. Second, to
keep the lamps burning.
To be dressed ready for service was the attitude that Jesus had in serving
God and people. While on earth, Jesus was dressed ready to serve. He served all kinds of
people by teaching the word of God, healing the sick and driving out demons. Jesus
served his disciples by sharing common life with them and showing them the secrets of
the kingdom of God. Jesus was ready to serve anyone and anywhere. Jesus urges us to
be dressed ready for service.
Another person who was dressed ready to serve was Abraham in Genesis. One
day, he was sitting at the entrance of his tent in the heat of the day when he noticed
three strangers coming. He hurried to meet them and bowed low to the ground, ready to
serve them. He hurried to Sarah and asked her to bake some bread. He ran to the herd
and found a choice, tender calf for the main course and prepared it. He also brought
yogurt and fresh milk and served a most delicious meal. As they ate, he stood by, ready
to meet their every need. Abraham was an old man, but he ran around like a young man
to serve his guests. Abraham didn’t know who these men were. It turned out that it was
the Lord with two angels (Ge 17:1). Referring to what Abraham did, this is what the Bible
says: “Do not forget to show hospitality to strangers, for by so doing some people have

be dressed ready for service as we wait for his return.
Jesus also tells us that we must keep our lamps burning as we wait for his
return. This is much more than to turn the switch on for our porch light. In Jesus’ time,
to keep lamps burning, servants had to prepare oil and fill the lamps before the wicks
burned out. They had to be prepared and vigilant so that the oil did not run out and the
light did not go out. So Jesus is telling us that we should be prepared, ready and
available to serve the Lord while we wait for his return. We must keep the light of Jesus
burning in our lives. We must keep the light of Jesus shine in this dark world, by living a
life of faith that reflects the love of God. We must share the word of God with young
people until the morning star rises in their hearts that are darkened by sin.
So Jesus wants us to be ready for his second coming. But how certain is his
coming? After all, he has not returned for 2,000 years, when the first-century Christians
thought he would come back in their life time. Let us make a couple of things very clear
here. We can be 100% certain Jesus is coming back. It is a promise of God that is written
down in the Bible. And God never reneged on his promise. We can be sure that he will
keep his promise of Jesus’ second coming. But we do not know the timing of his coming.
No one does. Not even angels. Not even Jesus himself. Only the Father knows (Mk
13:32). And the Bible says that God is delaying the timing because he wants one more
person to repent and be saved before the day of the Lord. St. Peter says: “The Lord is not
slow in keeping his promise, as some understand slowness. Instead he is patient with
you, not wanting anyone to perish, but everyone to come to repentance” (2Pe 3:9).
Just to make sure you know, let me quote several Bible verses about the promise
of Jesus’ second coming. Jesus told his disciples that he would come again in power and
glory to judge the living and the dead. He said in Luke 9:26, “If anyone is ashamed of me
and my words, the Son of Man will be ashamed of him when he comes in his glory and
in the glory of the Father and of the holy angels.” 1 Thessalonians 4:16-18 says, “For the
Lord himself will come down from heaven, with a loud command, with the voice of the
archangel and with the trumpet call of God, and the dead in Christ will rise first. After
that, we who are still alive and are left will be caught up together with them in the clouds
to meet the Lord in the air. And so we will be with the Lord forever. Therefore encourage
each other with these words.” Revelation 1:7 says, “Look, he is coming in the clouds, and
every eye will see him ....”
Knowing that Jesus is coming back and we don’t know the time, it is important
for us to be ready. And this is the main thrust of Jesus’ teaching in today’s passage. Look
at verses 37-38. “It will be good for those servants whose master finds them watching
when he comes. Truly I tell you, he will dress himself to serve, will have them recline at
the table and will come and wait on them. It will be good for those servants whose
master finds them ready, even if he comes in the middle of the night or toward
daybreak.” Those who are watching for the Master’s return and are ready for his coming
will be blessed. The blessing they receive is really amazing. The Master will dress
himself to serve them and let them recline at the table and wait on them. This is

Look at verse 39. “But understand this: If the owner of the house had known at
what hour the thief was coming, he would not have let his house be broken into.” The
Bible declares that “the day of the Lord will come like a thief” (2Pe 3:10). A thief would
not rob a house by giving the owner a heads-up. Jesus will come like a thief at the hour
no one expects. If we knew the exact time in advance, many of us would live a sinful life
until the last minute. But Jesus warns us that his coming will be as sudden and
unexpected as that of a thief in the night. No one knows when he will come. If anyone
tells you they know the time, they are lying (Mt 24:36). Since we do not know, we must
always be ready. Let’s read verse 40 together. “You also must be ready, because the Son
of Man will come at an hour when you do not expect him.” May God help us to be
dressed ready for service and to keep our lamps burning until Jesus comes again.
Look at verse 41. Peter asked, “Lord, are you telling this parable to us, or to
everyone?” Peter asked Jesus to clarify something. Who must be dressed ready for
service and keep the lamps burning? The disciples like himself or everyone? In his
answer, Jesus made it clear that everyone must be ready for his return, but those who
are in leadership positions will be held accountable. In verses 42-46, Jesus contrasts two
servants who were entrusted with the task of feeding God’s servants. The first one is a
faithful and wise manager. A faithful man is a trustworthy man. If he is given a task, he
will carry it out to completion, no matter what the cost. This man was not only faithful,
but also wise. He understood people well. He knew how to motivate coworkers and how
to solve difficult problems. He was creative and inspirational. The task he was given was
to feed the servants under him at the proper time. Here, “feeding” refers to giving the
word of God for the nurture of their souls. The best way to prepare for Jesus’ Second
Coming is to teach the word of God to his children. This is God’s wisdom. But
sometimes it is difficult. In this troubled world, we seem to need all our wits and
strength just to survive and to help our children survive. Some people in history thought
that the way to prepare Jesus’ Second Coming was to go to mountains and live like a
hermit. But a truly wise man studies the Bible and teaches the Bible to others for their
everlasting salvation. That is why Daniel 12:3 says, “Those who are wise will shine like
the brightness of the heavens, and those who lead many to righteousness, like the stars
for ever and ever.”
On the other hand, there was a servant who said, “My master is taking a long time
in coming.” He was smart at least in his mind. He figured out that it would be a while
before the master returned. So he abused his leadership by beating the other servants,
and indulged in physical pleasure. He thought he could enjoy a corrupted life for a
while, thinking he had plenty of time to change his life. But when he indulged in

Look at verses 47-48. “The servant who knows the master’s will and does not get

one who does not know and does things deserving punishment will be beaten with few
blows. From everyone who has been given much, much will be demanded; and from
the one who has been entrusted with much, much more will be asked.” Jesus is very
clear. We who know the gospel must be faithful and wise managers for the work of God
as we wait for Jesus’ coming.
On a visit to NASA, President Kennedy noticed a janitor who was hard at work in
sweeping the floors and complemented him for being a best janitor he had ever seen.
The janitor replied, “Sir, I am helping to put a man on the moon.” This man had a
purpose in what he did, even though he cleaned the floors, so he did his best in what he
did. I think this is very important for us who serve the Lord as we wait for his return.
Many things we do seem small and insignificant, such as studying the Bible 1:1 with
students or preparing lunch for our Sunday fellowship, or setting up tables for a
meeting. But if we do them before the Lord with a sense of serving him, we can do so
joyfully and with a best effort.
II. KNOW HOW TO INTERPRET THE TIMES (49-53)
Look at verse 49. “I have come to bring fire on the earth and how I wish it were
already kindled!” This is a scary statement about the judgment of God. The humanity
was destroyed by God during the Great Flood, except for the eight members of Noah’s
family. Then God promised that never again he would judge the world with water. From
the scriptures, we know that the world will be judged by fire instead. Revelation 21:8
declares: “But the cowardly, the unbelieving, the vile, the murderers, the sexually
immoral, those who practice magic arts, the idolaters and all liars--they will be
consigned to the fiery lake of burning sulfur. This is the second death.” I don’t mean to
scare you, but do you know how painful a burn injury is? I learned that on the scale of 1
to 10, the pain from delivering a baby for the first time is 7.5, while you would feel 8.0
when your arm or leg is cut off. But the most severe pain comes from being burned
(8.5). There is no other pain more severe than this. You get only 5.0 from cancer. So
imagine being thrown into the fiery lake of burning sulfur in eternity. We should not
take this warning lightly. We should know that Jesus said this not only because it is the
truth but also his heart was broken for those who refused to repent their sins and accept
the Savior.
Jesus wanted to establish the kingdom of God on earth as it is in heaven as soon
as possible. But first he had to suffer, die and rise from the dead. Jesus’ death and

resurrection would set believers free from the power of sin and death. And he would
come back to reign with us in the kingdom of God forever. But sadly, not everyone
would believe. That is why Jesus said he came not to bring peace to the world but a
sword (Mt 10:34-36). We know Jesus does not want us to use a sword. He rebuked Peter
and told him to put away his sword, “for all who draw the sword will die by the sword”
(Mt 26:52). So what did Jesus mean? Actually, Jesus came to bring peace to us, by dying
for our sins and restoring our relationship with God (Ro 5:1). But those who reject the
gospel of salvation through Jesus remain at war with God. They do not have peace. And
there is always conflict between those who believe in Christ and those who reject him,
even among family members.
Jesus turned again to the crowd. They knew how to forecast the weather based on
cloud activity and wind conditions. But they did not know how to interpret the present
time. They failed to see the humanity under curse because of sin. Jesus rebuked them in
verse 56. “Hypocrites! You know how to interpret the appearance of the earth and the
sky. How is it that you don’t know how to interpret this present time?” They saw time
and again how Jesus demonstrated God’s power, love and mercy by healing the sick,
driving out demons, and most of all, preaching the kingdom of God. But they did not
repent their sins and accept the Savior. Jesus knew these very people would soon be
shouting, “Crucify him! Crucify him!” Jesus was broken-hearted. He appealed to them
in verse 57, “Why don’t you judge for yourselves what is right?” Jesus really wanted
them to make a decision based on the facts and what their own consciences taught them
about the present time. Jesus really wanted them to accept him as the Messiah and
make a decision to live by faith in him.
In order to help them further, Jesus urged them to work things out before it was
too late by telling them a parable. Look at verses 58-59. “As you are going with your
adversary to the magistrate, try hard to be reconciled on the way, or your adversary
may drag you off to the judge, and the judge turn you over to the officer, and the
officer throw you into prison. I tell you, you will not get out until you have paid the last
penny.”
In today passage, we learned that Jesus is coming again at an hour when we do
not expect him. We must be ready. We must serve God and others with the attitude of
Jesus on a daily basis. We must teach the word of God to others as faithful and wise
managers of God’s work. May God help us to live each day with the hope of reigning
with our Lord Jesus Christ in everlasting glory in the kingdom of God!
