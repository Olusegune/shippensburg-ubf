THE PROMISE OF THE HOLY SPIRIT
John 14:15-31
Key Verses: 14:15-16
“If you love me, keep my commands. And I will ask the Father, and he will give
you another advocate to help you and be with you forever—”
Last week, we learned that Jesus is the only way to the kingdom of God. John
14:6 says, “I am the way and the truth and the life. No one comes to the Father except
through me.” Jesus is the only way, because he opened the way to heaven through his
death on the cross and his resurrection on the third day.
In today’s passage, Jesus gives us the promise of the Holy Spirit. Who is this Holy
Spirit? What can the Holy Spirit do for us? How can we receive the Holy Spirit? Let us
open our hearts and hear the words of Christ!
I. THE HOLY SPIRIT WILL BE WITH US FOREVER
Look at verses 15-17. “If you love me, keep my commands. And I will ask the
Father, and he will give you another advocate to help you and be with you forever—
the Spirit of truth...” Jesus’ disciples were anxious and troubled because Jesus had told
them that he was leaving. They had separation anxiety. Ever since they became his
disciples, Jesus took care of them. He taught them the secrets of the kingdom of God; he
showed them his compassionate heart toward sinners and his divine power by
performing many miracles. He traveled with them everywhere he went. Now he told
them that he was going away. What would they do without Jesus? They felt like they
would be abandoned as orphans (18). How did Jesus help them?
Look at verse 16 again. “And I will ask the Father, and he will give you another
advocate to help you and be with you forever.” Jesus promised to send them the Holy
Spirit. But Jesus used another name for the Holy Spirit. He would ask God to send
“another advocate to be with them forever.” Other Bible versions translates “advocate”
as “Counselor” (HCSB) “Comforter” (KJV) or “Helper” (ESV). Until now, Jesus had been
their advocate, their guidance counselor for three and a half years, teaching them what is
right and what is wrong. Now Jesus promised them that he would send the Holy Spirit
to be with them forever. They did not have to feel anxious. They did not have to suffer
from separation anxiety, because Jesus would be with them. Jesus would be with them
and guide them through the Spirit. In verse 18, Jesus told them, “I will not leave you as
orphans; I will come to you.” This promise is for us as well. When we accept Jesus as
our Savior and commit our lives to him, he comes to us through the Holy Spirit to dwell
in our souls. So we are not alone; we are not orphans. The Holy Spirit is with us to guide
us through all the ups and downs in life.
At this point, let us think about who the Holy Spirit is. In verse 16, Jesus calls the
Holy Spirit “another advocate” or “another Counselor.” Why did Jesus say that God
would send “another” Counselor? It is because Jesus is the Counselor. Up until now,

Jesus was with them as their Counselor. Jesus helped them. He comforted them. Now
Jesus was going away. Physically, he wouldn’t be with them any longer. But he would be
with them in the Spirit. He would send another Counselor, the Holy Spirit. There are
several things we need to know about the Holy Spirit.
First, the Holy Spirit is a person, just as God is a person and Jesus is a person. We
are mistaken if we identity the Holy Spirit only with a strong wind or a fire. The Holy
Spirit is not merely a vehicle through which God’s power is manifested. The Holy Spirit
is a person who can communicate with us and help us personally. As a counselor, the
Holy Spirit guides us to live a life of faith in God. The Holy Spirit guides us to make a
right decision in times of uncertainty. Just as we can talk to God in prayer, we can talk to
the Holy Spirit. Just as we can hear Jesus’ voice personally as we study his words, we
can hear the Holy Spirit through the word of God. There are many men and women of
God in history who communicated with the Holy Spirit.
After Jesus’ death and resurrection, a great persecution broke out against the
believers in Jerusalem. Stephen became the first martyr. All except the apostles were
scattered through Judea and Samaria. Among them was Philip who went down to a city
in Samaria to preach the gospel of Jesus. The response in the city was great (Ac 8:1-8).
There was a great joy. Then the Holy Spirit told Philip to leave the successful ministry
and go down to a desert road leading to Gaza. Humanly this did not make sense. Why
abandon a fruitful ministry and go to a desert area? But Philip was a humble man. He
listened to the Holy Spirit rather than his own logic. He obeyed the voice of the Holy
Spirit. And the result? He met an important official from Ethiopia. They had a 1:1 Bible
study on his chariot. The official was baptized and became a Christian. Historians
believe that this was the reason why Ethiopia was the first Christian nation in Africa.
Second, the Holy Spirit dwells in each of us who believes in Jesus Christ.
Jesus says in verse 17: “… the Spirit of truth. The world cannot accept him, because it
neither sees him nor knows him. But you know him, for he lives with you and will be in
you.” The world refers to those who do not accept Jesus. The world cannot accept the
Holy Spirit. It neither sees the Holy Spirit nor knows him. But the Holy Spirit lives with
everyone who accepts Jesus as his or her personal Savior. The Holy Spirit is in each of
us. He lives in us. Jesus also says in verse 20: “Before long, the world will not see me
anymore, but you will see me. Because I live, you also will live.” The world cannot see
Jesus, but we can see Jesus because the Holy Spirit dwells in us. The world does not
acknowledge Jesus, but we acknowledge him as our Lord and Savior because the Holy
Spirit convicts our hearts. Again, the Holy Spirit indwells in each of us. He is a gentle
person. He does not force us to do anything. So if we want to follow his guidance, we
must be sensitive to his gentle nudge. We should be humble. We should pray. Then the
indwelling Spirit guides us through the word of God and also through conviction in our
hearts to follow the ways of God. This indwelling of the Holy Spirit in each believer is
different from the filling of the Spirit in a person by God for a specific task, but that is a
topic for another day.
Third, the Holy Spirit is God. To be exact, the Holy Spirit is the third person in God.
This truth about trinity is confusing to many people. A man sarcastically said, “If God is

the Father and Jesus is the Son, then who is God’s wife?” Many people do not
understand about the three persons in one God. It is not easy to understand this if we try
to grasp it with our human logic. God is infinite. He is beyond our logic and
imaginations. We must acknowledge the infinite wisdom and power of God as we
approach him. Then God will surely help us to know him. Although the word “trinity”
does not appear in the Bible, the references about the three persons in God are
unmistakable. In Genesis 1, God said, “Let us make man in our image, in our likeness,…”
(Ge 1:26). After his resurrection, Jesus gave the Great Commission, saying, “Therefore
go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of

the Corinthian believers, “May the grace of the Lord Jesus Christ, and the love of God,
and the fellowship of the Holy Spirit be with you all” (2Co 13:14). We learn that God
functions in his divinity in three ways: God the Father as the Sovereign Ruler; God the
Son as the Savior of the world; and the Holy Spirit as the invisible God who dwells in the
souls of those who love God and his Son Jesus Christ.
Fourth, the Holy Spirit teaches us the truth. As the Counselor, Comforter and
Advocate, the Holy Spirit has many ways in helping and guiding us. He convicts us about
our sin so that we may repent (16:8). He helps us to have a conviction about our
salvation and hope of eternal life. Most of all, he guides us into all truth (16:13). In verse
17, Jesus calls the Holy Spirit “the Spirit of truth.” The Holy Spirit reveals the truth, that
is, he reveals to us the truth about God and Jesus. Look at verse 26. “But the Advocate,
the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you all things and
will remind you of everything I have said to you.” The Holy Spirit can help us to really
understand the words of Christ. He can help us to know the heart of Christ. This is the
reason why we should pray for the help of the Holy Spirit every time we study the Bible.
When the Holy Spirit is our teacher, we can understand and obey Jesus’ teaching from
our hearts.
When we call on God, we are calling God the Father, God the Son and God the
Holy Spirit all at once. God is like a loving father, Jesus is like a loving mother, and the
Holy Spirit is a gentle counselor. It is important for us to be sensitive to the indwelling of
the Holy Spirit in our souls. When the Spirit dwells in us, we never feel alone.
II. “IF YOU LOVE ME, KEEP MY COMMANDS”
Look at verses 15-16 again. “If you love me, keep my commands. And I will ask
the Father, and he will give you another advocate to help you and be with you
forever...” Who can receive the Holy Spirit? Jesus says that those who love him can
receive the Holy Spirit. Those who reject Jesus and do not love him cannot receive the
Holy Spirit. To reject Jesus is to reject the Holy Spirit.
Look at verse 15 again. Jesus says, “If you love me, keep my commands.” Jesus
repeats this teaching two more times in this passage. He says in verse 21, “Whoever has
my commands and keeps them is the one who loves me.” Once again, Jesus says in verse
23, “Anyone who loves me will obey my teaching.” The message from Jesus is very
clear. If we love Jesus, we will obey his words. Let us think about this more deeply. First,

love is the motivation in obeying Jesus. We love him and therefore we obey his words.
Why do we love him? We love him because he first loved us (1Jn 4:19). We love Jesus
because he gave his life on the cross in order to save us from eternal condemnation.
Jesus paid the price of our sins so that we may be free from the bondage of sin and
death. We love Jesus knowing that he loves us.
Then what does it mean to love Jesus? How can we love Jesus? We cannot love
Jesus by simply reciting, “I love you, Jesus,” no matter how many times we say it. We
can love Jesus by doing what he tells us to do. Love and obedience go hand in hand. Our
obedience is the evidence that we love Jesus. If we really love someone, we would be
glad to do something that the person is pleased with. So it is important for us to study
carefully the words of Christ and obey his teaching in our lives. What does Jesus want
from us? What is the heart of God? What can we do to please our Lord Jesus?
You may find many answers to this question. But when you study the gospels
carefully, there is one command of Jesus that stands out. All four gospels end with this
command of Jesus. It is the Great Commission of Jesus. He told his disciples to go and
make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son
and of the Holy Spirit. And this command is not just for pastors and missionaries. It is
for everyone who follows Jesus--everyone who loves him. Clearly, taking care of God’s
sheep is one way for us to show our love for Jesus. There is a wonderful dialogue
between Jesus and his disciple Peter about this in John 21:15-17: When they had
finished eating, Jesus said to Simon Peter, “Simon son of John, do you truly love me
more than these?” “Yes, Lord,” he said, “you know that I love you.” Jesus said, “Feed
my lambs.” Again Jesus said, “Simon son of John, do you truly love me?” He answered,
“Yes, Lord, you know that I love you.” Jesus said, “Take care of my sheep.” The third
time he said to him, “Simon son of John, do you love me?” Peter was hurt because Jesus
asked him the third time, “Do you love me?” He said, “Lord, you know all things; you
know that I love you.” Jesus said, “Feed my sheep.” Jesus raised up his disciples so they
could take care of his sheep as he had done during his ministry on earth.
Look at verses 21-22. Whoever has my commands and keeps them is the one who
loves me. The one who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I too will love them
and show myself to them.” Then Judas (not Judas Iscariot) said, “But, Lord, why do
you intend to show yourself to us and not to the world?” Judas’ question revealed the
disciples’ frustration. They wanted Jesus to show himself to the world. They wanted him
to stop talking about suffering and dying. What did Jesus say to them in response? Jesus
said, “Anyone who loves me will obey my teaching. My Father will love them, and we
will come to them and make our home with them. Anyone who does not love me will
not obey my teaching. These words you hear are not my own; they belong to the
Father who sent me” (23-24). Jesus tells us that not everyone is open to his word, let
alone loves him and obeys his teaching. Since the first century, the gospel has been
preached to millions of people but not everyone is receptive to the message. Our Father
will love and dwell with only those who obey Jesus’ word.
Jesus promises us that if we love him and obey his teaching, he and the Father
God will love and make their home with us. When the Holy Spirit dwells in a person, he

or she can be changed into a new person. Indeed, the disciples of Jesus were changed by
the Holy Spirit. Peter had bragged about his human loyalty to Jesus when he said, “I will
lay down my life for you!” But in his fear, he denied Jesus three times before a little girl.
Then the rooster crowed. At this moment Peter remembered what Jesus had said, “I tell
you, Peter, before the rooster crows today, you will deny three times that you know me”
(Lk 22:34). The Holy Spirit reminded him of what Jesus had said to him. Then Peter
cried in repentance. And Peter became a new person, especially after he received the
Holy Spirit at Pentecost. He was no longer fearful. He encouraged his fellow Christians
to participate in the remaining suffering of our Lord Jesus Christ. He encouraged them
to obey Jesus’ world mission command. Finally, he was arrested and about to be
crucified. But he was not fearful at all. According to tradition, he told the executioners,
“Our Lord Jesus Christ was crucified. I do not deserve to die as the way our Lord Jesus
Christ did. Please crucify me upside down.”
Look at verse 27. Jesus said to the troubled disciples, “Peace I leave with you; my
peace I give you. I do not give to you as the world gives. Do not let your hearts be
troubled and do not be afraid.” At the beginning of this chapter, Jesus told his disciples:
“Do not let your hearts be troubled. You believe in God; believe also in me” (1). This
time, Jesus said, “Peace I leave with you; my peace I give you.” He promised them the
peace of God. The peace that the world gives is relative and temporary. For example,
several peace treaties were signed between Israel and Arab nations, but they still do not
have peace. You may have peace in your family when things go well. This peace may
disappear quickly if you lose your job or someone gets sick. But the peace of God is
different. It is the peace we have in our hearts, knowing that our sins have been forgiven
(Ro 5:1) and that Jesus has prepared a place for us in heaven. It is the peace in our
hearts, knowing that Jesus is with us. Only Jesus can give us this peace.
Look at verses 28-29. “You heard me say, ‘I am going away and I am coming
back to you.’ If you loved me, you would be glad that I am going to the Father, for the
Father is greater than I. I have told you now before it happens, so that when it does
happen you will believe.” Jesus reaffirms his promise that he would come back again.
Jesus also gives a warning that before his glorious Second Coming, the prince of this
world would come (30). “The prince of this world” refers to Satan. Jesus crushed Satan’s
head by dying on the cross for our sins and by rising from the dead. But Satan still has
the arrogance to try to tempt and ensnare God’s people. But we don’t have to worry. As
long as we love Jesus by obeying his words and allow the Holy Spirit to dwell in us,
Satan has no power over us.
In today’s passage, we learned that even though Jesus is not here physically, he is
with us through the Holy Spirit, reminding us of his words and counseling us to follow
the ways of God. When the Holy Spirit is with us, we can have the peace of God. May
God help us to have an intimate fellowship with the Holy Spirit everyday! May God help
us to love Jesus by obeying his words!
