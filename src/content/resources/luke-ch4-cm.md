THE TEACHING AND HEALING MINISTRY OF JESUS
Luke 4:31-44
Key Verse: 4:32
They were amazed at his teaching, because his words had authority.
Today’s passage teaches us that the beginning of Jesus’ ministry was focused on
two things: preaching the word of God and healing the sick. Let is think about why Jesus
started his ministry by teaching the word of God. May God also help us to learn how
Jesus healed sick people with his personal touch on each person!
First, his words had authority (31-37). Look at verses 31-32. Then he went down to
Capernaum, a town in Galilee, and on the Sabbath he taught the people. They were
amazed at his teaching, because his words had authority. On the Sabbath, Jesus taught
the people the word of God. The main focus of his ministry was teaching the word of
God. The people at that time were suffering from various economic, political, social
problems under the oppression of the Roman Empire and King Herod. Teaching them
the word of God did not seem practical. Humanitarian work would have been much
more appealing. But Jesus taught them the word of God. Why? The word of God is no
ordinary word. The word of God can make us wise for salvation through faith in Christ
Jesus (2Ti 3:15). The word of God is the word of life. Jesus taught the people the word of
God so that they might come to know God and receive eternal life. In John 6, we learn
that Jesus fed a crowd of 5,000 hungry people with only 5 loaves of bread and 2 fish out
of his compassion for them. They were so impressed by the miracle that they wanted
make Jesus king. But Jesus didn’t come to this world to become a free baker. He came to
save us from our sin and death. So when a crowd of people came to him the next day, he
didn’t give them free bread. Instead, he told them, “Do not work for food that spoils, but
for food that endures to eternal life, which the Son of Man will give you” (Jn 6:27). Jesus
also declared, “I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never go hungry, and
whoever believes in me will never be thirsty” (Jn 6:35). The people were not happy
because Jesus refused to give them free bread. Many of them left him, grumbling. So
Jesus turned to his twelve disciples and asked, “You do not want to leave too, do you?”
Simon Peter answered him, “Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal
life” (Jn 6:68).
We have many problems in this world and in our personal lives. Sometimes we
wish that a political savior would come to solve all our problems. But no one can help us
with our spiritual problems—the problems of sin and death. We must realize that the
ultimate answers to our life problems can be given only by God. He alone can save us
from our bondage of sin and death. We can find the answers to our problems when the
word of God comes to our hearts. Jesus has the words of eternal life. This is the reason
why we devote ourselves to the study of the word of God.
There are many people who have no word of God in their hearts. As a result, they
have no spiritual sense of right or wrong. They just follow the trend of this world. For
example, the Bible teaches us that sex outside marriage and homosexuality are both

From Jesus we learn that we must also share the word of God with young people in our

How did people respond to Jesus’ teaching? Look at verse 32 again. They were
amazed at his teaching, because his words had authority. They were amazed at his
teaching, because his words had authority. What does it mean that his words had
authority? The word “authority” is usually associated with a position of power in society.
Cumberland County supervisors have the authority to tax your property. State troopers
have the authority to pull you over on I-81 and ask you, “How fast you think you’re
going. Let me see your driver’s license.” However, the term “authority” here is different
from such worldly power. Jesus’ words had authority because they were the words of
God. Mark says Jesus taught with authority, “not as the teachers of the law” (Mk 1:22).
Jesus did not quote several prominent rabbis. He did not talk about the fine details of
the Sabbath law such as you should not travel more than 5/8 of mile or carry anything
heavier than 2 dried figs. Instead, Jesus taught them the word of God directly. As I said,
the word of God is no ordinary word. The word of God is alive and active. It is sharper
than any double-edged sword. It penetrates even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and
marrow. It judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart (Heb 4:12). The word of God
can change a person’s heart. It can transform a person’s life. Josh McDowell, a popular
Christian author and evangelist, was a frustrated teenager in rural Michigan. He wanted
to be happy and to have a life with meaning. He had three questions in life that no one
seemed to give him the answers: Who am I? Why am I here? Where am I going? Then
God led him to study the word of God in college and he became a Christian. Someone
asked him, “How do you know you became a Christian?” He answered, Christ “changed
my life.” He confessed that Jesus was more than a carpenter. The word of God has
transforming power!
The human Jesus had absolute faith in the word of God. It was his weapon to
fight against Satan’s temptations. Jesus had fasted for 40 days, when Satan came to
tempt him in the desert with three things to which all humans are vulnerable: First,
bread to satisfy the hunger; second, a shortcut to glory and success; third, an easygoing
way to fulfill God-given mission. Each time, Jesus defeated the devil’s temptation with
the word of God (4:1-13).
So people were amazed at his teaching. And something happened to further
convince them how his words had authority. Look at verses 33-34. In the synagogue
there was a man possessed by a demon, an impure spirit. He cried out at the top of his
voice, “Go away! What do you want with us, Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to

destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One of God!” A demon or an evil spirit is
Satan’s field agent. Some people think that there is no such thing as an evil spirit in this
scientific age. Others are afraid of demons. Still others see lucrative potential in this
subject, exploiting people’s fear and imaginations. Movies and books dealing with this
subject made millions of dollars. What does the Bible say about this? We learn that
demons are real. The Bible says that demons could cause mental disorder (Jn 10:20),
violent action (Lk 8:26-29), bodily disease (13:11,16), and rebellion against God (Rev
16:14). Many people in this world are filled with fear and anxiety, as Satan plants fear in
their hearts. Fear and doubt are Satan’s weapons of choice.
The demon in this passage tried to interrupt Jesus’ teaching the word of God, like
a hackler. The demon was unhappy because people were listening to the word of God
from Jesus. He cried out at the top of his voice: “Go away! What do you want with us,
Jesus of Nazareth? Have you come to destroy us? I know who you are—the Holy One
of God!” The demon knew that Jesus is the Holy One of God, when many people did not
know Jesus. Satan is smarter than we are. After all, he was originally an angel that
rebelled against God. Satan knows God but he refuses to worship God. Instead, he
himself wants to be worshipped. His intention is to tempt us to join him in his rebellion
against God to suffer eternal death with him. Satan is a murderer and a liar from the
beginning (Jn 8:44).
The demon also knew that Jesus had come to destroy him. He knew that Jesus
would not allow him to continue to torment the man. How did Jesus deal with the
demon inside the man? Look at verse 35a. “Be quiet!” Jesus said sternly. “Come out of
him!” The demon could not but obey the Messiah’s command. But he didn’t want to give
up his victim easily. The demon threw the man down before them all and then came out
without injuring him (35). Jesus’ words had authority over demons!
We learn that Jesus cared deeply about this one man who was suffering under the
power of the demon. Nobody paid attention to the pathetic man, only calling him ‘the
crazy man.’ But Jesus stopped his preaching in order to take care of the demon-
possessed man. Jesus is a shepherd who cares for one lost sheep. How did the people
respond to his healing of the man? Look at verses 36-37. All the people were amazed
and said to each other, “What words these are! With authority and power he gives
orders to impure spirits and they come out!” And the news about him spread
throughout the surrounding area. The people were very impressed by Jesus’ words of
authority and power over evil spirits. They witnessed something amazing. Jesus cast out
the demon from the man. Jesus came to cast out demons. This is good news to us as
well. Even today, many people suffer from the torment of the devil. Some people refuse
to worship God, thinking that they can be their own men. But the truth is that, when
they are away from God, they come under the influence of the devil. Those who have no
love relationship with God are under the demon’s rule. There is no middle ground. You
cannot ride the spiritual fence. But when we have a love relationship with God, we can
have beautiful love relationships with others. When we come to Jesus and accept his
word, we can experience new life, new joy and a new vision in life. We don’t have to
worry about demons possessing us as long as we are in Christ.

Second, Jesus heals Simon’s mother-in-law (38-39). Look at verse 38. Jesus left
the synagogue and went to the home of Simon. Now Simon’s mother-in-law was
suffering from a high fever, and they asked Jesus to help her. We don’t know exactly
how Simon’s mother-in-law got sick. Perhaps it was caused by her worries about her
son-in-law. Simon had been a hardworking fisherman. But she heard that lately he was
spending more time with this country preacher, a carpenter from Nazareth. Perhaps she
began to worry about the financial future of her daughter’s family. Anyway, when Jesus
arrived at Simon’s house, she was suffering from a high fever. No one seemed to
understand her sorrow and agony of life as a widow who had to live in her married
daughter’s house. But Jesus understood her. Look at verse 39. So he bent over her and
rebuked the fever, and it left her. She got up at once and began to wait on them. Jesus
had rebuked the demon. Now he rebuked the fever and it left her. His words had
authority over a disease! How did she react to the healing touch of Jesus? She got up at
once and began to wait on Jesus and his disciples, serving them with a delicious meal. In
Jesus’ time, widows had little or no chance of remarrying. They had no economic means.
So widows lived a sorrowful life. Jesus comforted Peter’s mother-in-law and made her
well so that she could serve the mission team. Jesus is the God of compassion. In Jesus,
an old woman can live a blessed life of serving the Lord.
Third, Jesus heals many people, one by one (40-41). Look at verse 40. At sunset,
the people brought to Jesus all who had various kinds of sickness, and laying his hands
on each one, he healed them. As soon as the Sabbath was over, there was a constant
stream of people who came to him. They had various kinds of sickness. Old people with
arthritis, men with chronic back pains, women suffering from hemorrhage, children
with ear infections, and even teenagers with severe zit problems were brought to him.
Today’s medical doctors would have closed their offices at five o’clock sharp and tell the
people to call for an appointment. Amazingly, Jesus cared for the sick people one by
one. Laying his hands on each one, he healed them.
One UBF doctor told me how difficult it is to see dozens of patients everyday.
Jesus was very tired. But he was filled with compassion and healed the sick one by one
until everyone received a personal care. Most likely Jesus worked well into the night to
take care of them all. This same Jesus is with us today as well. He is no longer confined
by time and space. He is sitting at the right hand of God. He knows our personal
struggles and cares for us. There are over 7 billion people in the world today. But Jesus
does not see them as too many people. He sees each one of us as one who needs his
personal touch. We are not some numbers in the eyes of our shepherd Jesus. He cares
for each one of us, one by one, if we are willing to let him. We also learn from Jesus that
we should take care of our Bible students, one person at a time, until he or she is healed
through the word of God, until each one comes to know the Savior Jesus personally.
Look at verse 41. Moreover, demons came out of many people, shouting, “You
are the Son of God!” But he rebuked them and would not allow them to speak, because
they knew he was the Messiah. The demons shouted, “You are the Son of God!” They
knew he was the Messiah. But Jesus didn’t say, “Thanks for recognizing me. I appreciate
it.” Instead, he rebuked them and did not allow them to speak. Jesus didn’t need the

Fourth, Jesus prays early in the morning (42-44). Look at verse 42. At daybreak,
Jesus went out to a solitary place. The people were looking for him and when they
came to where he was, they tried to keep him from leaving them. Jesus had worked
hard until late into the night. But the next morning he got up early and went to a solitary
place. Why did he go to a solitary place? Mark 1:35 says: Very early in the morning,
while it was still dark, Jesus got up, left the house and went off to a solitary place,
where he prayed. Jesus could find no time to pray because of the constant stream of
sick people. But he found time to pray early in the morning. Through the quiet time of
prayer, the human Jesus listened to God. He also pleaded with God. Most of all, he
renewed his love relationship with God. Some people consider prayer as a shopping list
of things they want from God. Prayer is not a one way communication line. We should
not call God, tell him what we want from him, and then hang up, without asking him
what he wants from us. Prayer is time for us to maintain a close relationship with God.
Prayer is the measure of faith, because if we don’t pray to God, we are telling him we
don’t need him.
While Jesus was praying, the people were looking for him. When they found him,
they tried to keep him from leaving the town. They wanted him to keep on healing the
sick people. Perhaps they wanted to build a big hospital so that Jesus could establish a
medical mission there. They were excited about having a free physician in town. What
did Jesus say to them? Look at verse 43. But he said, “I must proclaim the good news of
the kingdom of God to the other towns also, because that is why I was sent.” Through
his early morning prayer, Jesus found a clear direction for his ministry. The healing
work was going well. He was getting popular among the people. But they needed healing
of their sin-sick souls more than their bodies. He said, “I must proclaim the good news
of the kingdom of God to the other towns also, because that is why I was sent.”
From today’s passage we learn that Jesus’ words have authority because it is the
word of God. Jesus healed the sick, laying his hands on each one. May God help each of
us to share the word of God with young people of our time so that they may be healed by
the healing touch of our Lord Jesus!
